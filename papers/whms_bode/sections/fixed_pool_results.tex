\subsection{Fixed-pool coverage and false positives}
\label{sec:fixedpool}\label{sec:lowcounts}
Two standards answer different questions (Table~\ref{tab:precision}). At
$s=0.5$, percentile coverage is below the historical minimum of 90\% in
three conditions; SMC/reference reaches 91.0\% [90.2, 91.8]. All four,
including SMC/reference, remain below the intervals' nominal 95\% level,
with every Wilson upper endpoint below 95\%. The historical tolerance
does not establish nominal calibration.

At $s=1$, the fixed-pool target is zero, so null rejection is exactly
one minus coverage among defined intervals. The reported 9.8--32.2\%
null rejection is therefore 67.8--90.2\% null coverage, rather than an
independent calibration criterion. Coverage is lower at the null than
at the halving in all four conditions. Detection at $s=0.5$ is a distinct
quantity: exclusion of zero under a nonzero effect, here 47.5--76.2\%.
All detection upper endpoints are below the historical 80\% requirement;
all null-rejection lower endpoints exceed nominal 5\%. Wilson intervals
quantify Monte Carlo precision conditional on the source pools.

\subsection{Shared-reference scoring removes part of the estimation error}
\label{sec:sharedreference}
At 800 cells, evaluating the percentile intervals against the sampled
reference gives 98.9--99.6\% inclusion, compared with 92.3--93.6\%
coverage of the fixed-pool effect. The paired differences are 5.8--6.8
percentage points with Monte Carlo standard errors of 0.33--0.36 points.
Only the scoring target changes. At five cells the direction reverses;
shared randomness need not inflate inclusion at every sample size.

The increasing discrepancy at large tumor-arm counts has an analytic
explanation. The estimate is $\hat\theta=f_N(\bar Y_T-\bar Y_N)$, whereas
its error against the drawn benchmark is
$\hat\theta-i_{\mathrm{req}}=f_N(\bar Y_T-s\bar Y_N)$.
For independent arm draws conditional on $P$, their error variances differ by
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta-\theta_P\mid P)
-\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta-i_{\mathrm{req}}\mid P)
=f_N^2(1-s^2)\frac{\sigma_P^2}{n_N},
\label{eq:variancegap}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_P^2$ is the source-pool expression variance and $n_N=800$.
At $s=0.5$, the drawn benchmark removes three quarters of the reference-arm
variance contribution from the error being assessed, while the bootstrap
continues resampling both arms. This explains why near-perfect benchmark
inclusion can accompany lower coverage of the generating parameter. Skewed
finite-sample errors still determine the actual percentile-interval rates.

These precision runs assess four specified counts, without selecting a
replacement threshold. The eight-seed grid below remains scored against
its original benchmark and cannot be reinterpreted as fixed-pool calibration.
