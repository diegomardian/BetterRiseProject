\subsection{Fixed-pool coverage is lower at the reporting boundary}
\label{sec:fixedpool}
At 50 cells, fixed-pool coverage is 86.5\% and 91.0\% for SMC pooled and
reference-only draws, and 87.0\% and 84.5\% for KUL3. The corresponding
95\% Wilson intervals are [81.1,90.6], [86.2,94.2], [81.6,91.0] and
[78.8,88.9]\%, respectively. The first three leave uncertainty about the
90\% requirement; KUL3/reference is below it even at the upper endpoint.
With discrimination of 52.5\%, 78.0\%, 54.0\% and 55.0\%, none of the four
50-cell conditions meets both empirical criteria in this seed stream.

Table~\ref{tab:fixedpool} separates target choice from changes in the estimator
or interval. At 800 cells, drawn-reference inclusion is 99.0--100.0\%, but
fixed-pool coverage is 90.5--94.0\%. The paired decreases are 6.0, 9.5, 8.0
and 7.0 percentage points for SMC/pooled, SMC/reference, KUL3/pooled and
KUL3/reference; their Monte Carlo standard errors are 1.68, 2.08, 1.92 and
1.81 points. At five cells, switching targets can instead raise inclusion.
Thus the target change has a measurable effect whose direction depends on
sample size; neither score can substitute for the other.

The increasing discrepancy at large tumour-arm counts has an analytic
explanation. The estimate is $\hat\theta=f_N(\bar Y_T-\bar Y_N)$, whereas
its error against the drawn benchmark is
$\hat\theta-i_{\mathrm{req}}=f_N(\bar Y_T-s\bar Y_N)$.
For independent arm draws conditional on $P$, their error variances differ by
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta-\theta_P\mid P)
-\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta-i_{\mathrm{req}}\mid P)
=f_N^2(1-s^2)\frac{\sigma_P^2}{n_N},
\label{eq:variancegap}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_P^2$ is the source-pool expression variance and $n_N=800$.
At $s=0.5$, the drawn benchmark removes three quarters of the reference-arm
variance contribution from the error being assessed, while the bootstrap
continues resampling both arms. This explains why near-perfect benchmark
inclusion can accompany lower coverage of the generating parameter. Skewed
finite-sample errors still determine the actual percentile-interval rates.

The fixed-pool analysis covers four counts and one seed stream. We therefore
retain the eight-seed grid selections as benchmark-based results and do not
reinterpret them as fixed-pool calibrated cutpoints.
