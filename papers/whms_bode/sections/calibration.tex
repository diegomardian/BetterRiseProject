\section{Empirical audit: when recovery fails to validate a reporting rule}
\label{sec:calibration}

\paragraph{Application and reporting policy.}
The empirical application separates two explanations for differentiation-marker
loss in colorectal cancer: fewer marker-producing cells (a \emph{compositional}
change), or lower output from the cells that remain (an \emph{intrinsic}
change). These explanations suggest different biological interpretations.
An empirical comparison of per-cell means requires the relevant population
to be observed in both tissues. Let $f_N,f_T$ denote the relevant cell fractions
in reference and diseased tissue, and $m_N,m_T$ their mean per-cell output.
A reference-based decomposition, related to demographic standardisation and
Oaxaca--Blinder decompositions \citep{kitagawa1955,oaxaca1973,blinder1973}, is
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{(f_T-f_N)m_N}_{\text{compositional}} +
\underbrace{f_N(m_T-m_N)}_{\text{intrinsic}} +
\underbrace{(f_T-f_N)(m_T-m_N)}_{\text{interaction}}
= f_Tm_T-f_Nm_N.
\label{eq:kitagawa}
\end{equation}
The interaction is reported separately. If no relevant cells are observed in
an arm, its sample mean is undefined; the identity cannot supply the missing
mean. In a synthetic population with no such cells, the corresponding
conditional mean itself has no referent. Substituting zero in either case
turns an unavailable contrast into an apparent effect.

The implemented reporting rule returns an estimate at $n\geq50$, flags a wide
interval at $20\leq n<50$, and returns \notest{} below 20, where $n$ is the
relevant-cell count in diseased tissue. Unavailable reported contrasts are
stored as \texttt{None}, not zero. The diagnostic oracle calculations examined
below also retain raw arithmetic before this reporting gate. We evaluate the boundary directly and retain pre-gate intervals at smaller
counts to diagnose the curve's behavior.

\paragraph{The claim being re-evaluated.}
An internal assessment (Artifact A) treated the 50-cell boundary as validated
because recovery ratios were 0.85--1.08 above it and 1.18 in the 20--49-cell
band, using six draws per setting at 1{,}200 cells per arm. The design record
(Artifact B) instead required at least 90\% interval coverage and 80\% exclusion
of zero at a halving of expression. Appendix~\ref{app:provenance} and the
anonymized supporting record make this mismatch inspectable.

\subsection{Simulation and inferential targets}
The design document fixed a detectable halving of per-cell output ($s=0.5$),
coverage of the target in at least $90\%$ of defined outputs, and discrimination of at least $80\%$, defined as the probability
that the interval excludes zero at that effect. The implementation scored coverage against a drawn-reference benchmark;
we retain that score and add coverage against the generating pool mean.
The candidate $ok$ is the first evaluated count or bin meeting both criteria;
$wide$ is the first meeting coverage alone. The committed values are 50 and 20.
The first-crossing definition does not require larger counts to qualify.

The calibration uses paired subsets of the published colorectal atlas
\citep{lee2020}: ten patients from GSE132465 (SMC) and six from
GSE144735 (KUL3). We analyse GUCA2A in the differentiated population under fixed
lineage labelling. Each draw samples, with replacement, 2{,}000 cells per arm
from an eligible two-patient pool; the reference mature fraction is $f_N=0.40$.
The implemented pool combines cells from both tissues before drawing either
synthetic arm. Because the design document did not specify tissue eligibility,
we also evaluate a reference-tissue-only pool. Both arms sample the same eligible pool, with an imposed expression shift. A nominal 95\% percentile interval resamples mature cells in
both arms, with fractions held fixed and 200 inner draws unless stated otherwise.
Undefined intervals are excluded from rate denominators and reported as
abstentions (Appendix~\ref{app:gridcaveats}).

The stored ``parametric'' intrinsic reference is
\begin{equation}
i_{\mathrm{req}}(D_N;s)=f_N(s-1)\bar Y_N,
\label{eq:requested}
\end{equation}
where $\bar Y_N$ is the mature-cell mean of the \emph{drawn} reference arm.
Let $P$ be the eligible two-patient source pool, $M_P$ its mature-cell count,
and $Y_j$ its unshifted target-gene counts. The population parameter of this
finite empirical distribution is instead
\begin{equation}
\mu_P=\frac{1}{M_P}\sum_{j\in P,\,\mathrm{mature}}Y_j,
\qquad \theta_P(s)=f_N(s-1)\mu_P.
\label{eq:fixedpool}
\end{equation}
We compute $\mu_P$ from all eligible source cells before either synthetic arm
is sampled. Given $P$, the generator has $E[\bar Y_N]=\mu_P$ and
$E[\bar Y_T]=s\mu_P$, making Equation~\ref{eq:fixedpool} its true intrinsic
mean contrast. It is fixed across cell draws from that pool. Patient-pair
selection changes $P$; reported coverage averages this conditional-pool
question over the selected pairs. An all-patient pooled mean would use a
different population from the generator's two-patient draw. Neither target
supplies inference over new patients.

Two complementary runs separate historical selection from direct evaluation.
The controlled eight-seed grid retains inclusion of
Equation~\ref{eq:requested}. An initial 200-replicate diagnostic reproduced
its first-seed results and added fixed-pool coverage and null rejection.
The precision follow-up uses 5{,}000 fresh studies per cohort, pool, count
(5, 50, 100, 800) and shift ($s=0.5,1$), split across five seed streams:
160{,}000 studies in total. It samples the exact marginal law of mature-cell
counts in the original generator; non-mature cells do not enter this contrast.
On each study we compare the original 200-resample percentile interval with a
Welch--Satterthwaite interval \citep{nistwelch}. This standard unequal-variance
$t$ approximation is a comparator, not an assumed solution for sparse counts.
All 320{,}000 intervals are finite. The largest binomial Monte Carlo standard
error is 0.71 percentage points, conditional on the empirical source pools.
Appendix~\ref{app:precision} specifies the sampling and interval calculations.

\subsection{Count grids change candidate cutpoints}
Historical grids returned reference-only binned candidates of 100, 90 and 70,
but changed random streams, bin boundaries and replicate budgets together
(Appendix~\ref{app:historical}). To isolate these choices, we simulated the
union of counts once per cohort, pool and each of eight seeds, with 200 outer
replicates and 200 inner bootstrap draws. Every grid summary reuses the same
outputs at shared counts. A \emph{grid} is the set of mature-cell counts a
sweep can evaluate, fixed by which mature-cell fractions it varies. The
committed grid is the one recorded in the original design and reaches
$\{0,20,40,100,200,400,800\}$; the filled and dense grids add support between
and below those counts, and differ from it in nothing else.
Fixed bins share boundaries across grids; adaptive bins recalculate them.
Binned candidates are median counts within the selected bin, not estimated
crossing locations.

With common fixed bins, SMC returns 100 on the committed grid and 70 on both
expanded grids in all eight seeds. Adaptive bins return 65 on seven filled-grid
seeds and 90 on one, while the dense grid returns 70 throughout. KUL3 instead
returns 400 on six seeds and 800 on two, with all grid and binning schemes
agreeing within each seed. Pooled draws yield no joint candidate in either
cohort. The largest binomial Monte Carlo standard error in this grid is 0.035.

At the committed count of 50, SMC reference-only discrimination ranges
from 75.5\% to 81.0\% across seeds, with both criteria met on two of eight
seeds. KUL3 reference-only discrimination is only 45.0--55.0\% at that count.
The reporting boundary therefore misses the discrimination requirement in
six of eight SMC seeds and every KUL3 seed.

The per-count summaries in Table~\ref{tab:controlledgrid} supply a useful
check on the bin medians. In SMC, the filled grid's first qualifying actual
count is 50 on two seeds, 60 on five and 80 on one; the dense grid adds a
candidate of 45 on one seed. Thus a stable binned candidate of 70 is not
evidence that 70 is a uniquely determined biological threshold. A bin pools
outcomes over several count settings, whereas a per-count rate addresses only
that setting. Fixed bins hold the boundaries constant, but adding settings can
still change both a bin's median count and its aggregate performance.

\subsection{First qualification can reverse at larger counts}\label{sec:reversals}
Three of eight SMC/reference seeds in the complete saved union grid fail a
criterion after first qualifying. This matters for
a rule that reports all counts above a scalar boundary: passing at its first
point does not ensure performance over its reporting range. Requiring every
subsequently evaluated point to qualify changes candidates 45, 50 and 50 to
60, 70 and 70. No reversal occurs in the eight KUL3/reference seeds; no pooled
candidate qualifies. These are descriptive selections on the recorded benchmark.


\subsection{Patient influence and the cohort gap}
Enumerating all 45 and 15 two-patient holdout pairs separates patient omission
from repeated simulation. Keying streams to the sorted pair identifiers keeps
every retained pair fixed. Raising the inner bootstrap budget from 200 to 1{,}000
and 5{,}000 leaves the first cohort's candidate at 70, but moves the second's
400 to 300 and back to 400. Omitting one source patient changes the joint
coverage-and-discrimination candidate for none of ten patients in the first
cohort and three of six in the second (KUL01, KUL30 and KUL31). For the
coverage-only candidate, the corresponding counts are five of ten and zero of
six. These checks quantify influence within each observed cohort.
Appendix~\ref{app:guards} gives the biological comparability limits.


The sensitivity windows cover counts $40,50,60,70,80,90,100,110$ in SMC and
$200,300,400,600,800$ in KUL3. These per-count summaries use a fixed pair
schedule, so they are a separate experiment from the eight-seed union grid.
Omitting KUL01 or KUL30 moves the joint candidate from 400 to 300; omitting
KUL31 moves it to the lowest tested count, 200, with its lower boundary
unobserved. The coverage-only baselines likewise occur at the lowest tested
counts, 40 and 200. Their stability cannot locate a lower crossing outside
those windows. Overlapping pairs do not add independent patients.

\paragraph{Biological interpretation is a separate requirement.}\label{sec:biocontrols}
Interval calibration cannot establish a biological mechanism. The assumed
retained-gene control, MS4A12, instead falls by $-0.971$ to $-0.951$ in
non-sparse primary-cohort strata. Its depletion persists under broader cell
inclusion and depth matching and appears in a separate bulk dataset.
These checks undermine the panel's retention assumption; they do not separate
composition from within-cell change. Appendix~\ref{app:controls} reports this
control-panel audit as a limitation of the biological application.
