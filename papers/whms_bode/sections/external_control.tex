\section{Population alignment in clinical simulation}
\label{sec:eca}
A clinical simulation must also name the population whose effect is evaluated.
In a constructed external-control example, the intended target $\tau_E$ is
the average individual treatment effect among enrolled records. Averaging
stratum contrasts over the pooled trial-plus-control population instead
estimates a different target. Scoring that estimator against the same pooled
contrast gives perfect agreement by construction. More informatively, a
pooled potential-outcome reference breaks the identity yet still favors the
wrong-population estimator; scoring against $\tau_E$ reverses the ranking.
Under zero case-mix shift the mismatch nearly disappears. This separates
target alignment from numerical recovery; Appendix~\ref{app:eca} gives the
simulation, controls and results.
