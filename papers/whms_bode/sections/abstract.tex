\begin{abstract}
Scoring the same nominal 95\% intervals against a sampled reference gives
98.9--99.6\% inclusion, versus 92.3--93.6\% coverage of the generating-pool
effect, in a single-cell simulation audit at 800 cells. An exact variance
calculation explains the discrepancy: under a halving of expression, sharing
the sampled reference mean removes three quarters of the reference-arm
variance contribution from the error being assessed. We trace the audit to
a 50-cell reporting rule previously justified by point-estimate recovery.
In 5{,}000 studies per setting, the original interval rejects a true null
in 9.8--32.2\% of studies; a Welch control without bootstrap resampling noise
still rejects in 8.9--30.8\%. A further 32{,}000-study comparison evaluates
an interior point of the flag-wide band, larger bootstrap budgets, BCa and
bootstrap-$t$. None of the tested methods satisfies coverage, detection and
nominal null calibration jointly at 30 or 50 cells. A short external-control
illustration distinguishes population alignment from numerical agreement.
The evidence is conditional on empirical cell pools: it identifies a failed
reporting claim and tests concrete repairs, without validating a replacement
threshold or a patient world model.
\end{abstract}
