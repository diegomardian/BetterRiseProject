\begin{abstract}
Recovering a requested effect in simulation does not establish that an
interval or an abstention rule meets its operating requirements. We examine
an instance in which a single-cell recovery curve was used to justify a
cell-count threshold. Direct evaluation on two small colorectal single-cell
cohorts does not support that justification: the implemented pooled-cell
simulation yields no qualifying cutpoint, while reference-only draws produce
candidates sensitive to count aggregation and cohort composition. Reusing
identical draws across grids isolates the contribution of count support and
binning. An equality audit explains the narrower question answered by the
original curve: its oracle estimate reproduces a realised empirical reference.
This equality does not cause the calibration failure or imply an invalid
estimator. A control gives the same zero-residual point estimate approximately
95\% or 36\% coverage by changing only its interval. Stratified patient and
survival simulations demonstrate how equivalent comparisons arise in
trial-effect estimation and depend on the reference, assignment design and
observation process. Learned generators preserve the identities in the tested
saturated estimators; a limited search of seven public repositories finds no
functional reuse under its classification rule. We propose reporting the
reference and residual as an audit of what a comparison measures, alongside
separate evaluation of target error, interval performance and abstention.
\end{abstract}
