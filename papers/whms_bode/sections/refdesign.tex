\section{Worked checks: what changes an empirical comparison?}
\label{sec:matrix}

The following examples use established relationships to check three choices:
the reference, the observation process and the fitted estimator. They are
implementation evidence for interpreting a recovery curve, not new equivalence
theorems. Appendix~\ref{app:methods} gives the simulation settings.

\paragraph{Choose the reference before interpreting a residual.}
For stratum counts $n_g$ and arm means $\bar Y_{ag}$, standardisation uses
$T(D)=\sum_g(n_g/n)(\bar Y_{1g}-\bar Y_{0g})$.
Saturated G-computation \citep{robins1986} equals this functional by construction.
Empirical-propensity IPW also equals it when both arms are nonempty in each
stratum: weighting the treated mean by $1/\hat p_g$, with
$\hat p_g=n_{1g}/n_g$, gives $(n_g/n)\bar Y_{1g}$, and similarly for controls.
The augmentation terms of non-cross-fitted saturated AIPW sum to zero within
stratum; matching with all controls recovers the same stratum means.

OLS with additive stratum indicators instead weights stratum contrasts by
normalised $n_g\hat p_g(1-\hat p_g)$ \citep{angrist1998}, obtained by
residualising treatment and outcome on stratum indicators
\citep{frisch1933}. Table~\ref{tab:matrix} checks these identities numerically.
Under confounding, G-computation and OLS switch between zero and 0.0663
residual when the reference changes. Complete 1:1 blocks make both weights
proportional to $n_g$, whereas Bernoulli assignment balances only in expectation.
The result is an instruction for interpreting the comparison: specify its
weights and assignment constraints before calling two recovery curves independent
evidence. The observed $10^{-14}$ residuals verify the implementation.

\paragraph{Distinguish observed and latent quantities.}
With administrative censoring, the standardised exponential estimator uses
events divided by exposure. Against that same observed-data functional its
residual is exactly zero; against the corresponding latent-time functional,
the maximum at 3{,}000 patients rises from zero without censoring to 0.376 at
78\% censoring. The estimator has not changed; the comparison now includes
information hidden by censoring. Conversely, standardised Kaplan--Meier RMST
at $\tau=2$, below every censoring horizon, agrees with the empirical latent
truncated-mean contrast to $2.8\times10^{-15}$. Censoring outside the target
horizon leaves this functional observable. Nonfinite fits are excluded in
pairs; the most affected setting loses 463 of 1{,}200 draws
(Appendix~\ref{app:cox}). A larger latent residual does not rank estimator quality.

\paragraph{Separate generator complexity from estimator complexity.}
Saturated G-computation reproduces the drawn reference in all 30 learned-generator
and 12 parametric-control settings because the equality holds for each dataset,
regardless of its generator. A mixture M-step likewise has
$\pi_k=n_k/n$ and $\mu_k=\sum_i r_{ik}y_i/n_k$, implying
$\sum_k\pi_k\mu_k=\bar y$. These are explanatory identities, not tests of
patient-model fidelity. Table~\ref{tab:learned} shows their scope: ridge and MLP
outcome fits can depart, with an MLP maximum of 3.3 on mixture-generated data.
The run contains 75{,}580 evaluations and 74{,}726 finite drawn-reference pairs.
Of 420 settings, 41 ridge settings change numerical classification when
relative tolerance is added. This demonstrates why the raw residual, outcome
units and tolerances must accompany any closeness label
(Appendix~\ref{app:learned}).

\paragraph{External evidence bounds the claim.}
The pre-registered search found no functional reuse in seven classified public
repositories and no examinable code for its virtual-control-arm query.
This is neither a representative prevalence estimate nor corroboration of a
widespread failure mode. The positive evidence in this paper is the documented
internal case; the negative search result limits its external reach
(Appendix~\ref{app:prevalence}).
