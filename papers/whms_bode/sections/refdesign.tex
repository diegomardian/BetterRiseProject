\section{Reference dependence in trial-effect simulations}
\label{sec:matrix}

The case study concerns a biological decision rule; the following experiments
examine the same distinction in stylised trial-effect estimation. Their targets
are fixed generating parameters. These simulations establish possible
identities, not their frequency in clinical practice.

\paragraph{A stratified patient simulator.}
For stratum counts $n_g$, total sample size $n$, and observed arm means
$\bar Y_{ag}$, define
\[
T(D)=\sum_g\frac{n_g}{n}(\bar Y_{1g}-\bar Y_{0g}).
\]
Saturated G-computation \citep{robins1986} calculates this contrast directly.
IPW with empirical propensities $\hat p_g=n_{1g}/n_g$ also equals it: each
stratum's treated weighted sum divided by $n$ is
$(n_g/n)\bar Y_{1g}$, and likewise for controls. This requires nonempty arms
within each stratum and these specific weights. It is not an equivalence for
arbitrary outcome or propensity models. The reference is an observed contrast,
not the finite-sample mean of paired potential-outcome differences.

Figure~\ref{fig:trial} evaluates five estimators in three covariate strata with
stratum-dependent treatment probabilities, a homogeneous additive effect
$\theta=3$, and cohorts of 100--5{,}000 patients. Four estimators recover the
requested effect and tighten with cohort size. Two reproduce $T(D)$:
G-computation has exactly zero residual and saturated IPW agrees to numerical
precision. Cross-fitting the IPW propensity \citep{chernozhukov2018} breaks the
equality in this design because each patient's weight is fitted on a different
fold. It remains consistent here, but its nonzero residual establishes only
departure from this reference. The unadjusted contrast is confounded, with a
recovery ratio near 2.4; the recovery curve correctly reveals that error.

Ordinary least squares (OLS) with treatment and additive stratum dummies also
departs from $T(D)$, although it uses every record and is correctly specified
for this homogeneous effect. Standardisation weights strata by prevalence;
OLS uses normalised weights proportional to $n_g\hat p_g(1-\hat p_g)$.
Its residual is smaller than cross-fitted IPW's at every tested cohort size.
Thus the audit concerns a shared functional, not simply a shared sample.

\paragraph{Assignment and the chosen reference change the equality.}
The sweep crosses 14 estimators, three designs, two realised references, six
cohort sizes and six seeds (settings in Appendix~\ref{app:methods}).
Table~\ref{tab:matrix} shows nine of those estimators under two of the
designs. Independent Bernoulli assignment is discussed below; the five
logistic-propensity variants are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:matrix}. Under confounded
assignment, OLS has maximum residual $0.0663$ against the standardised
reference at $n=5{,}000$, but $3.5\times10^{-14}$ against the
variance-weighted reference equal to OLS by the Frisch--Waugh identity.
G-computation reverses the comparison: $0$ and $0.0663$, respectively.
Both consistently estimate the requested homogeneous effect.

With complete, stratified permuted blocks and 1:1 allocation, empirical
propensities are $0.5$ in every stratum. The two references coincide, and
OLS and even the unadjusted contrast reproduce them to numerical precision
($1.6\times10^{-14}$ and $6.7\times10^{-15}$). Independent Bernoulli
assignment at probability $0.5$ does not enforce this realised balance:
at $n=5{,}000$, OLS and the unadjusted contrast have maximum residuals
$9.51\times10^{-4}$ and $0.562$, respectively, against the standardised
reference. Thus 1:1 randomisation in expectation is insufficient for the
identity. Non-cross-fitted AIPW with
saturated nuisances also equals standardisation: its within-stratum
augmentation sums vanish (residual $4.4\times10^{-15}$ in the confounded
design). Exact matching using all controls within each stratum agrees to
$8.9\times10^{-16}$; using 20 or one control gives residuals of $0.052$ and
$0.164$. These are properties of the implemented estimator, reference and
design together. Appendix~\ref{app:matrix} also examines regularisation and
effect heterogeneity.

\paragraph{Censoring changes the comparison.}
We repeated the patient experiment with exponential event times, confounded
assignment and administrative censoring: four censoring levels, seven cohort
sizes, and 1{,}200 replicates pooled over 6 seeds per cell. Paired comparisons
exclude non-finite outputs, including event-free cells and Cox fitting failures.
Exclusions occur even at low censoring and remove $463$ of $1{,}200$ draws at
$78\%$ censoring and $n=100$; reported residuals are conditional on valid pairs.

The per-stratum exponential maximum-likelihood estimator uses rates equal to
events divided by exposure, then averages log rate contrasts by stratum
prevalence. Its observed-data reference is that same functional, giving maximum
residual zero in all 28 cells. Against the corresponding latent-time reference,
maximum residuals at $n=3{,}000$ are $0$, $0.061$, $0.144$ and $0.376$ at
$0\%$, $12\%$, $49\%$ and $78\%$ censoring. The worst values across cohort
sizes are $0$, $0.473$, $1.089$ and $1.586$. The estimator is unchanged;
the latent comparison measures a different departure, associated with
information hidden by censoring.

At $49\%$ censoring, the exponential estimator's maximum latent-reference
residual exceeds stratified Cox's in five of seven cohort sizes; at $78\%$, in
six of seven ($0.376$ versus $0.293$ at $n=3{,}000$). Per-replicate inversion
rates are $0.53$--$0.61$. Neither ordering is a quality criterion: a larger
residual cannot be read as a more informative validation. Use an observed-data
reference for the reuse audit, and retain requested parameters and latent
targets for their separate error, coverage and information-loss evaluations.

A restricted-mean survival time (RMST) comparison provides a control.
Its horizon is fixed at $\tau=2$, below every administrative censoring time
in the sweep. Standardised Kaplan--Meier RMST therefore reproduces the empirical
latent truncated-mean contrast to numerical precision across all 28 cells
(maximum residual $2.8\times10^{-15}$ on valid outputs). Censoring has hidden
no information within this horizon. The observation process matters through
its effect on the chosen functional, not simply through the fraction censored.

An independent check of eight Cox fits agrees with
\texttt{lifelines} to $1.4\times10^{-7}$, below the specified $10^{-5}$
tolerance (Appendix~\ref{app:cox}). It checks implementation agreement on one
cohort, not coverage or convergence across the sweep.

\paragraph{Learned generators preserve the tested identities.}
A further sweep fits Gaussian-mixture and MLP generators, samples synthetic
cohorts, and refits estimators on each cohort. Saturated G-computation exactly
reproduces the drawn reference in all 30 learned-generator and 12 parametric
control settings. This is an estimator identity that holds regardless of how
the cohort was generated; it does not establish generator fidelity. Penalised
outcome models instead yield nonzero residuals. Of 420 estimator-by-generator-
by-size settings, 41 pass a relative-plus-absolute numerical comparison but
fail an absolute-only one; all 41 use ridge penalties. These are tolerance
classifications, not evidence of worse statistical performance with more data.
Appendix~\ref{app:learned} reports the full design and the distinct model-based
reference; Appendix~\ref{app:scale} discusses residual scale.

Table~\ref{tab:learned} reports every estimator in the expanded run, including
the MLP outcome model whose maximum residual reaches 3.3 on mixture-generated
data. Saturated and mixture-outcome identities therefore should not be
extrapolated to arbitrary learned estimators. In a mixture M-step,
$\pi_k=n_k/n$ and $\mu_k=\sum_i r_{ik}y_i/n_k$ imply
$\sum_k\pi_k\mu_k=\bar y$; generator complexity cannot break this particular
mean-preservation identity. By contrast, numerical closeness depends on the
chosen tolerance: comparing residuals to zero uses absolute tolerance alone,
whereas a relative tolerance additionally scales with the reference magnitude.
The recorded run contains 75{,}580 estimator evaluations and 74{,}726 finite
drawn-reference pairs; Appendix~\ref{app:learned} reconciles the exclusions.

\paragraph{Evidence outside the constructed examples is limited.}
A pre-registered search classified seven public repositories and found no
functional reuse under its rule. Six compare with generating parameters or
quantities computed outside the observed sample; the remaining case involves
distinct functionals that agree on noiseless linear data. Adding outcome noise
separates them, illustrating the need to state the domain of an identity.
The search did not locate examinable code for its query specifically targeting
virtual control arms. This is neither a representative prevalence estimate nor
evidence that deployed systems are affected (Appendix~\ref{app:prevalence}).
