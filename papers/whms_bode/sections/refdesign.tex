\section{Supporting estimator and trial checks}
\label{app:matrix}\label{sec:matrix}

\paragraph{Reference dependence.}
With nonempty stratum arms, saturated G-computation \citep{robins1986}, IPW
using empirical stratum propensities, non-cross-fitted saturated AIPW and
matching using every control all reproduce
$T(D)=\sum_g(n_g/n)(\bar Y_{1g}-\bar Y_{0g})$. The IPW weights recover each
stratum mean; the AIPW augmentation sums to zero within stratum. OLS with
additive stratum indicators instead weights contrasts by normalized
$n_g\hat p_g(1-\hat p_g)$ \citep{angrist1998,frisch1933}. Under confounded
assignment at 5{,}000 patients, switching between these two references changes
the G-computation/OLS residuals from $0/0.0663$ to $0.0663/3.5\times10^{-14}$.
Complete 1:1 blocks make the weights coincide. These are implementation checks
of established identities. Under effect heterogeneity, OLS recovery rises
from 1.003 to 1.132 while its own-reference residual stays at numerical precision.

\paragraph{Interval control.}
For 200 independent $\mathcal N(\theta,1)$ observations and 2{,}000 draws per
effect, the full-sample mean has 94.60\% and 95.40\% coverage at
$\theta=0,0.5$. Quartering the interval half-width leaves the estimate and its
zero residual against the empirical mean unchanged, but reduces coverage to
35.95\% and 36.30\%. The control illustrates why residuals cannot certify
intervals.\label{tab:cleancontrol}

\paragraph{Censoring and implementation checks.}\label{app:cox}
The standardized exponential estimator equals its events/exposure reference.
At 3{,}000 patients its maximum departure from the latent-time reference grows
from zero without censoring to 0.376 at 78\% censoring. RMST at $\tau=2$, below
every censoring horizon, agrees with the empirical latent truncated-mean
contrast to $2.8\times10^{-15}$. The most affected setting excludes 463 of
1{,}200 pairs with nonfinite fits. Eight independent Cox comparisons agree
with \texttt{lifelines} to $1.4\times10^{-7}$ against a $10^{-5}$ tolerance.

\paragraph{Learned-generator design and exclusions.}\label{app:learned}
Seven generators (four Gaussian mixtures, an MLP conditional Gaussian and two
parametric controls), ten estimators, six sizes, six seed streams and 30
replicates schedule 75{,}600 evaluations on 7{,}560 cohorts. Generator failures
leave 75{,}580 evaluations on 7{,}558 cohorts; 74{,}726 drawn-reference pairs
are finite. The 854 exclusions comprise 410 nonfinite references and 731
nonfinite estimates, overlapping in 287 evaluations. Saturated G-computation
has zero residual in every setting because the identity holds on each drawn
dataset. Ridge and MLP outcome fits can depart; the largest mixture-generated
MLP residual is 3.3. Generator complexity by itself does not test an estimator
identity or establish fidelity to patients.

\paragraph{Tolerance and reference choice.}\label{app:scale}
Of 420 settings, 41 ridge settings pass only a relative-plus-absolute comparison.
Thirteen generator--estimator combinations switch into this category between
$n=100$ and $5{,}000$. The comparisons use $10^{-8}$ against zero and
$10^{-8}+10^{-5}|T|$ against the reference. Report raw maxima, outcome units
and both tolerances; distinguish a proved identity from agreement on tested
draws. The optional ratio of median departure to median reference error is
undefined at zero denominator and cannot rank estimators. Mixture model-target
references use 200{,}000 Monte Carlo observations; saved median standard
errors are 0.019--0.027. Parametric and MLP model-target references are analytic.
