## 1. MSI coverage is fine — the escalation resolves favourably

The brief flagged this as a decision that had to be made before looking at
results: *"if coverage in TCGA is thin, the pre-registered variable has to
change."*

**Coverage is 98.6%.** It is not thin.

| Variable | Annotated | Coverage | Levels |
|---|---|---|---|
| sex | 624 | 100% | 3 |
| age | 621 | 99.5% | — |
| site | 618 | 99.0% | 4 |
| **msi_status** | **615** | **98.6%** | 3 |
| stage | 598 | 95.8% | 4 |

MSI breakdown: **523 MSS, 76 MSI, 16 conflicting, 9 unannotated.**

Two caveats that matter more than the coverage number:

- **The subgroup is 76 patients, not 615.** Coverage was never the binding
  constraint — group size is. README already notes interaction contrasts need
  roughly four times the sample size of a main effect, and 76 vs 523 is
  unbalanced on top of being small. The pre-registered variable can stand, but
  it should be reported as an estimate with an interval, exactly as planned, and
  nobody should expect it to support anything further.
- **16 patients have contradictory MSI calls.** Status is derived per aligned-reads
  file, and those 16 carry both an MSI and an MSS call across their WGS and WXS
  files. They are labelled `conflicting`, not resolved by a rule. Assigning them
  would fabricate a label in the project's one pre-registered subgroup variable.

## 2. Three of the five technical factors do not vary

| Factor | Levels | Largest | Usable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| TSS (tissue source site) | 38 | 172 | yes |
| Plate | 29 | 48 | yes |
| Sequencing centre | **1** | 624 | no — constant |
| Analyte | **1** | 624 | no — constant |
| Vial | 3 | 618 | effectively constant |

Every COAD/READ RNA-seq aliquot came from one sequencing centre with one analyte
type. That is good news — two potential confounders are absent by construction
rather than by adjustment — and it means "sequencing batch" in this cohort means
**plate**, with TSS as the site term. The constant factors are reported as `NaN`
rather than as a null result, because "cannot be tested" and "tested and found
nothing" are different statements.

## 3. Plate explains more expression variance than any biological variable

PVCA-style: PCs covering 80% of variance (124 of them), each regressed on each
factor, weighted by the PC's share. **Read `excess_over_null`** — a factor with
many levels explains variance by construction, and the permutation null is what
that construction is worth.

| Factor | Levels | R² | Adjusted R² | Null R² | **Excess** | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **plate** | 26 | 0.172 | 0.138 | 0.040 | **0.132** | 0.010 |
| **TSS** | 24 | 0.121 | 0.088 | 0.037 | **0.084** | 0.010 |
| msi_status | 3 | 0.045 | 0.042 | 0.003 | 0.042 | 0.010 |
| site | 4 | 0.028 | 0.023 | 0.005 | 0.023 | 0.010 |
| stage | 4 | 0.012 | 0.007 | 0.005 | 0.007 | 0.010 |
| project (COAD/READ) | 2 | 0.006 | 0.005 | 0.002 | 0.005 | 0.010 |
| vial | 3 | 0.003 | −0.001 | 0.003 | **−0.001** | 0.683 |

**Plate is associated with three times more expression variance than MSI status,
and nearly twenty times more than stage.** That is the headline.

`vial` is the built-in negative control and behaves: excess −0.001, p = 0.68. A
method that found structure everywhere would have flagged it too.

These figures are **not a partition** and do not sum to 1. Plates sit inside
sites, sites correlate with project, and each number is a marginal association.
A true partition needs a joint mixed model, which is more machinery than
"document it" warrants.

## 4. Confounding: the two that matter

Bias-corrected Cramér's V with permutation p-values throughout — a 29×4 table
with most cells empty has no valid chi-squared null.

| Technical | Clinical | V | Null | Excess | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TSS | **project** | 0.971 | 0.043 | **0.928** | 0.001 |
| plate | **project** | 0.736 | 0.041 | **0.695** | 0.001 |
| TSS | site | 0.557 | 0.031 | 0.526 | 0.001 |
| plate | site | 0.441 | 0.033 | 0.409 | 0.001 |
| **plate** | **msi_status** | 0.211 | 0.040 | **0.170** | **0.006** |
| TSS | stage | 0.112 | 0.034 | 0.078 | 0.050 |
| plate | stage | 0.072 | 0.033 | 0.038 | 0.215 |
| TSS | msi_status | 0.000 | 0.051 | −0.051 | 1.000 |
| vial | any | ≤0.05 | — | ≤0.03 | ≥0.14 |

### TSS and project are almost the same variable (V = 0.97)

A hospital submits colon cases or rectal cases, not both. So **"COAD vs READ" and
"which institution" are not separable in this cohort.** This bears directly on
[open decision #4](../../docs/open_decisions.md), pool or stratify: adding a
project covariate does not adjust for site, because the two are nearly the same
column. Whatever is decided, it should be decided knowing that a COAD/READ
contrast is also an institution contrast.

### Plate is confounded with MSI status (V = 0.21, p = 0.006)

This is the one with teeth. MSI is the project's single pre-registered subgroup
variable; plate explains three times more expression variance than MSI does; and
the two are associated. **An MSI-vs-MSS expression contrast is partly a
plate contrast**, and because we do not batch-correct, that cannot be removed by
preprocessing.

Note TSS is *not* confounded with MSI (V = 0.000, p = 1.000). So this is a plate
phenomenon, not an institution one — consistent with MSI-status samples having
been processed in clustered submissions.

## 5. What follows from this — and what does not

**Do not batch-correct.** Invariant 4 stands and nothing here challenges it. The
right response to a measured confound is to carry it into the model and report
it, not to remove it from the data.

**For W3.6, the covariate lock.** Plate is not currently on the pre-specified
list (stage, age, sex, MMR/MSI, purity, site). On this evidence it has a stronger
claim than stage does. That is a decision for the team, and it needs care:
29 levels against 624 samples is a lot of degrees of freedom, so a random effect
or a coarser grouping is probably wanted rather than 28 fixed-effect dummies.
**Recorded as open decision #14 rather than decided here.**

**For any MSI subgroup analysis.** Report the plate association alongside it.
With 76 MSI patients and a plate confound, an unqualified MSI contrast will
overstate what the data supports.

**This does not touch the single-cell arm** or gate criteria G1–G4. It is a
statement about the TCGA bulk cohort's processing structure.

## Method notes

Clinical annotation comes from the GDC cases and files endpoints, cached to
`data/raw/gdc_clinical/` so the analysis re-runs offline. Three things the API
will get wrong if allowed to:

- **182 of 633 cases have more than one diagnosis**, including prostate, breast
  and liver. `select_colorectal_diagnosis` picks by organ; `diagnoses[0]` would
  hand some patients another organ's stage.
- **There is no `demographic.gender` field** — it is `sex_at_birth`. Requesting
  the wrong name returns nothing rather than erroring, so the column arrives
  silently empty.
- **MSI is file-level and can disagree within a case.** See §1.

Stage is harmonised across AJCC editions by dropping sub-stage letters
(`Stage IIIB` → `III`); the twelve values TCGA actually uses are pinned in a
parametrised test. Site splits at the splenic flexure, so transverse colon counts
as right — the other convention exists and would move ~21 patients, so it is
pinned too. `Colon, NOS` (110 cases) is its own level rather than being guessed
into left or right.

Rare levels below 5 samples are pooled to `other` before the variance analysis;
a level containing one sample is fitted perfectly and inflates R². Pooling took
plate from 29 levels to 26 and TSS from 38 to 24, and both counts are reported.

39 new tests, 472 in the full suite, `ruff` clean. The load-bearing ones are the
null controls: `test_random_factor_explains_nothing_above_its_null` and
`test_independent_variables_are_not_flagged_as_confounded`. Without them a clean
result here would be indistinguishable from a broken analysis.
