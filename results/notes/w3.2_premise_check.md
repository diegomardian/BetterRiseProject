# W3.2 — Premise check: is bulk GUCA2A loss bimodal?

**Owner:** W3 · **Week:** 2 · **Branch:** `w3/premise-check` ·
**Status:** PROVISIONAL — not adjusted for tumour purity

Artifacts: `results/2026-08-17_cc06981/tcga_premise_bimodality.parquet` and
`w3.2_premise_distributions.png`. Code: [`src/bulk/premise.py`](../../src/bulk/premise.py).

---

## The verdict, in plain language

**GUCA2A loss in bulk colorectal tumours is continuous, not bimodal. There are
no two groups.**

The loss is real and it is large — the median COAD tumour sits at 2.8 log2(CPM+1)
against 9.0 in normal-adjacent tissue, roughly a 64-fold drop. But tumours are
spread smoothly across that whole range rather than falling into a "lost" pile
and a "retained" pile. Hartigan's dip test, which asks exactly the question
"is this one lump or more than one", returns p = 0.85 in COAD tumours and does
not come close to significance in any stratum we tested. The histogram shows the
same thing by eye: a broad flat smear from zero to seven, with no valley
anywhere in it.

CDX2 is a separate surprise. At the median it is **not lost at all** — 8.08 in
tumours against 8.31 in normal-adjacent, a difference of about 15%. It is also
unimodal (dip p = 0.95).

**What this means for the project:** the framing that sorts patients into
GUCA2A-lost and GUCA2A-retained does not have a natural cut point in the data,
because there is no gap to cut at. Per execution_plan.md §8.2, this is the
pre-registered consequence — *"if continuous, the two-type classification
dissolves into a regression."* Any downstream cut point is a modelling choice
imposed on a continuum, and should be reported as one.

## The numbers

log2(CPM+1). Dip test null is unimodality, so a **large** p-value means
"unimodal". `bic_delta` is BIC(1 component) − BIC(2), so positive favours two.

| Gene | Stratum | n | Median | Dip p | BIC Δ | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GUCA2A | COAD tumour | 458 | 2.79 | **0.851** | 39.9 | two Gaussians fit better, dip says unimodal |
| GUCA2A | READ tumour | 166 | 3.07 | **0.919** | 1.8 | continuous |
| GUCA2A | COAD+READ tumour | 624 | 2.84 | **0.982** | 55.3 | two Gaussians fit better, dip says unimodal |
| GUCA2A | COAD normal-adj | 41 | 9.00 | 0.961 | 16.6 | — |
| CDX2 | COAD tumour | 458 | 8.08 | **0.955** | 250.3 | two Gaussians fit better, dip says unimodal |
| CDX2 | READ tumour | 166 | 8.23 | **0.997** | 74.4 | two Gaussians fit better, dip says unimodal |
| CDX2 | COAD normal-adj | 41 | 8.31 | 0.917 | 4.5 | continuous |

READ normal-adjacent (n=10) is below the n=20 gate and reports
`insufficient_n` rather than a number that would look like an answer.

### The two tests disagree, and that is the finding

BIC prefers two components almost everywhere; the dip test rejects multimodality
nowhere. The brief anticipated exactly this and said to report both rather than
pick whichever agrees — so, plainly: **BIC is not evidence of two groups here.**
It compares "two Gaussians" against "one Gaussian", and a skewed unimodal
distribution is genuinely fit better by two. That is a statement about Gaussians,
not about patients. CDX2's BIC Δ of 250 alongside a dip p of 0.95 is the clearest
illustration: strongly non-Gaussian, entirely unimodal.

`tests/test_bulk_premise.py` pins this with a synthetic skewed-unimodal positive
control, so the distinction cannot quietly rot.

## Three ways this could be wrong

**Purity — the big one, and it is unresolved.** Bulk expression is diluted by
stromal and immune content, and none of the above is purity-adjusted. In
principle purity could *create* apparent structure; here it would have to do the
opposite — manufacture a smooth continuum out of two genuinely separated groups —
which is a harder thing for a confounder to do than the reverse. So the
continuous finding is probably robust to it. **This is an argument, not a
measurement.** Re-run conditioned on purity the moment W3.3 lands; the
`purity_adjusted` column is in the output as `False` so no one can read these
numbers as final.

**Zero inflation — ruled out.** log2(CPM+1) maps undetected samples to exactly
0.0, and a spike there plus a hump would be bimodal to any test. Only **0.2%** of
COAD tumours are zero for GUCA2A and 0% for CDX2, and every row is re-run on the
non-zero subset with materially identical results. Not the explanation.

**Normal-adjacent is thin.** 41 COAD and 10 READ against 624 tumours. The
tumour-side conclusions rest on n=458 and n=166 and are fine; the normal-side
medians are description, not inference, and READ's are barely even that.

## Method notes

Both tests run on log2(CPM+1) from `tcga_log2cpm_0.9.0.parquet`, seed 20260817.
Gaussian mixtures use `covariance_type="diag"` and `init_params="random_from_data"`
rather than the sklearn defaults — for one-dimensional data `diag` is
mathematically identical to `full`, and both changes keep the fit off a BLAS
Cholesky path. Reasoning is in the `_gmm_bic` docstring.

Panel genes are read here as **outcome variables**, which is what invariant 2
permits. Nothing in this module uses a panel gene to define a group, a label or
a reference.
