## Verdict: PASS

**Stage is prognostic on every endpoint.** The pipeline reproduces the
single best-known fact in colorectal cancer, which is what this task exists to
check.

| Endpoint | n | Events | Stage IV HR (vs I) | p | Log-rank across stages |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PFI** | 548 | 142 | **17.8** (7.5–42.1) | <1e-5 | 3.1e-25 |
| **DSS** | 532 | 67 | **61.4** (8.3–451.9) | 5e-05 | 3.1e-23 |
| OS | 550 | 110 | **11.1** (4.6–26.8) | <1e-5 | 2.2e-13 |

Hazard rises monotonically I → II → III → IV on all three, and the
Kaplan–Meier curves separate cleanly in the right order. Nothing upstream needs
investigating: the endpoint definitions, the joins and the censoring rules
behave.

Every model ran at or above the events-per-degree-of-freedom floor —
PFI 15.8, DSS 11.2, OS 12.2 — which is the payoff from excluding purity from
the clinical baseline in W3.6.

## The finding worth keeping: age separates OS from the other two

| Endpoint | Age HR per year | p |
|---|---|---|
| PFI | 1.001 | 0.93 |
| DSS | 1.017 | 0.11 |
| **OS** | **1.037** | **5e-05** |

**Age strongly predicts overall survival and does not predict progression or
disease-specific survival at all.** That is exactly the signature of non-cancer
mortality: older patients die more, of other things, and only OS counts those
deaths as events.

This is [invariant 9](../../CLAUDE.md)'s premise — *"COAD OS is contaminated by
non-cancer death"* — confirmed from our own cohort rather than taken on
authority. It also supports keeping OS secondary and, per
[decision #16](../../docs/open_decisions.md), leading with PFI.

## Proportional hazards: one violation, reported

| Endpoint | Term | Transform | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| PFI | `site[right_colon]` | km | 0.040 |
| OS | `site[right_colon]` | km | 0.013 |

Right-sided colon cancer's hazard relative to left is **not constant over
follow-up** — consistent with the literature, where right-sided disease carries
worse prognosis in advanced but not early stage. DSS does not show it, but DSS
drops site by design, so that is not independent evidence.

Not fixed, not worked around. A violated PH assumption is a finding — most often
a time-varying effect — and switching models until one behaves is how a result
gets manufactured. If site becomes a variable of interest rather than an
adjustment term, it should be stratified or given a time interaction, and that
would be a config change with a written reason.

Stage, age, sex and MSI show no violation on any endpoint.

## What the model says that is not the sanity check

Reported for completeness. None of this is a project result — these are
adjustment terms in a baseline fit.

- **MSI is not prognostic here** (HR 0.87–1.37, p 0.46–0.70 across endpoints).
  Consistent with its effect being modest and concentrated in stage II–III; with
  76 MSI patients this model was never going to resolve it, which is why the
  covariate lock treats MSI as an adjustment term rather than a powered test.
- **Sex is null throughout** (p 0.24–0.75).
- **DSS's stage IV interval is enormous** — 8.3 to 451.9. Stage I contributes a
  single DSS event. The point estimate is not meaningful at that width; the
  *ordering* is. This is the 67-event sparsity showing, and it is the reason DSS
  runs on a reduced covariate set.

## A bug worth recording

The first implementation of the PH check parsed
`CoxPHFitter.check_assumptions()`. **That method returns matplotlib axes, not
results** — so the parser found nothing, fell through to an empty table, and
reported "no term violates" while lifelines was printing two genuine violations
to stdout.

A check that cannot fail is worse than no check, because it produces a clean
line in a report. Now it uses `lifelines.statistics.proportional_hazard_test`
directly, and `test_ph_check_flags_a_genuine_violation` builds crossing Weibull
hazards and asserts the term is caught.

## Method notes

Stratified by project, so COAD and READ have separate baseline hazards — the
covariate lock's answer to decision #4, and it costs no degrees of freedom.
Reference levels come from the config (stage I, female, MSS, left colon), not
from pandas' alphabetical default, so a hazard ratio always means the same
comparison; `test_reference_levels_come_from_the_config` pins it.

`fit_cox` calls `require_locked` before fitting. The covariate set was confirmed
and locked in its own commit before any of these models ran, and a run against a
`proposed` set raises.

Complete case throughout, with attrition recorded per endpoint in
`tcga_baseline_attrition`.

15 new tests, 541 in the full suite, `ruff` clean.
