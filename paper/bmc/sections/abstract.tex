\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background.}
Validation checks in computational analysis pipelines state failure conditions
they do not always detect. We audit one single-cell differential-composition
workflow, in which a differentiation-marker change is decomposed into
compositional, cell-intrinsic and interaction terms with an explicit
not-estimable verdict, and we ask of each validation check whether it can detect
the failure it names.

\noindent\textbf{Results.}
Across seven cohorts, six generators and three interval methods, the percentile
bootstrap and BCa over-reject under every non-saturated generator, with median
false-positive rates of 7.0--8.6\% against a nominal 5\%, while the Student-$t$
interval is calibrated except under skew on the MLH1-methylated arm ($n=10$), at
7.0\%. A
cutpoint summary that requires both coverage and discrimination silently drops a
coverage-only crossing on both cohorts; reporting crossings as brackets recovers
it. Recomputing the adenoma decomposition across all weightings, rungs,
denominators and four scale-free statistics shows the primary portion is
identical to the committed table, and the load-bearing statistic separates all
eight cross-block contrasts at the claimed rung, while agreement across all four
statistics holds for six of eight under two weightings and five of eight under
the third. Two suppressed readings reproduce exactly from committed derived
inputs. The audit also found that the project's table resolver ordered same-date
runs by commit hash rather than by time: 23 table/date pairs disagreed and 9 held
different content, though no published number is wrong. The blinded challenge
that would validate the audit has not been executed.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions.}
Executable counterexamples expose validation checks that miss their stated
failure conditions while passing, and calibration and sensitivity analyses
delimit which descriptive estimates remain reportable. In this workflow the
reportable claim is a descriptive pattern in selected, depth-matched populations;
we do not establish cell-intrinsic silencing. The procedure transfers to other
pipelines, but its validation depends on a held-out challenge we have not yet
run.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
single-cell; validation; simulation; calibration; reproducibility
\end{abstract}
