\section{What the screen sees depends on the reference and the design}
\label{sec:matrix}

Whether an estimator reproduces the reference depends on the estimator, the
reference and the trial design together. Changing one can change the verdict.
The following sweep shows where the algebraic identities hold and how departures
change when realised balance or estimator fitting changes.

The sweep crosses 14 estimators, three designs, two realised references, six
cohort sizes and six seeds (settings in Appendix~\ref{app:methods}).
Table~\ref{tab:matrix} shows nine of those estimators under two of the
designs. Independent Bernoulli assignment is discussed below; the five
logistic-propensity variants are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:matrix}. Under confounded
assignment, OLS has maximum residual $0.0663$ against the standardised
reference at $n=5{,}000$, but $3.5\times10^{-14}$ against the
variance-weighted reference equal to OLS by the Frisch--Waugh identity.
G-computation reverses the comparison: $0$ and $0.0663$, respectively.
Both consistently estimate the requested homogeneous effect.

With complete, stratified permuted blocks and 1:1 allocation, empirical
propensities are $0.5$ in every stratum. The two references coincide, and
OLS and even the unadjusted contrast reproduce them to numerical precision
($1.6\times10^{-14}$ and $6.7\times10^{-15}$). Independent Bernoulli
assignment at probability $0.5$ does not enforce this realised balance:
at $n=5{,}000$, OLS and the unadjusted contrast have maximum residuals
$9.51\times10^{-4}$ and $0.562$, respectively, against the standardised
reference. Thus 1:1 randomisation in expectation is insufficient for the
identity. Non-cross-fitted AIPW with
saturated nuisances also equals standardisation: its within-stratum
augmentation sums vanish (residual $4.4\times10^{-15}$ in the confounded
design). Exact matching using all controls within each stratum agrees to
$8.9\times10^{-16}$; using 20 or one control gives residuals of $0.052$ and
$0.164$. These are properties of the implemented estimator, reference and
design together. Appendix~\ref{app:matrix} also examines regularisation and
effect heterogeneity.

\paragraph{Tested on a learned generator: the equality does not degrade, and
neither does the screen.} We fitted Gaussian-mixture and MLP generators to the
simulator, drew synthetic cohorts, and re-fitted the outcome model on each ---
the wiring a practitioner would build. Pre-registered with falsifiers before
the run (Appendix~\ref{app:learned}; 7 generators, 10 estimators, 6 cohort
sizes, 6 seeds). Saturated G-computation against the drawn reference returns
$\max|\hat\theta - T(\tilde D)| = 0$ \emph{bitwise}, in all 30 learned-generator
cells and all 12 parametric ones, unchanged across mixture capacity. That is the
paragraph above, measured: the generator's learnedness is not in the comparison,
so it cannot degrade it.

\textbf{The consequence is for our own remedy.} Where the residual is
identically zero, $\rho$ is zero for the same reason --- the residual is its
numerator --- so the scale statistic we recommend is blind in exactly the case
we recommend it for. Report $\rho$ as a descriptor of small \emph{nonzero}
residuals, never as a second opinion on an exact one.

\textbf{What does produce near-equality is penalisation, not learning.} Ridge
outcome models place the residual where the penalty puts it: $8.6\times10^{-5}$,
$8.6\times10^{-3}$ and $0.78$ at $\alpha = 10^{-5}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-1}$, on
learned and parametric generators alike. This matters because
\texttt{np.allclose} means two different things here:
$\texttt{allclose}(r, 0)$ reduces to its absolute tolerance, $10^{-8}$, since
the relative term multiplies zero, while $\texttt{allclose}(\hat\theta, T)$
gives $\approx 3\times10^{-5}$ at $\theta = 3$. Of 420 cells, 41 sit strictly
between the two, every one of them penalised. \textbf{And the gap widens with
data}: these residuals fall like $n^{-1.75}$, so fourteen arms that both screens
catch at $n = 100$ have slipped into the interval by $n = 5{,}000$. More
patients move a regularised estimator further into the blind spot, which is the
opposite of the usual reassurance.
