\paragraph{Scope and falsification.} Our colorectal-cancer instance supports
\emph{no biological or clinical claim}. Its pre-registered control panel failed
its own falsification rule: the retained-gene control fell inside the
compositional controls' range, while the intrinsic control was too sparse to
test the estimator. We therefore report methods and validation only. A second
cohort confirms the artefact, not the biology. A second route, evaluated
over three cohorts and 42 patients, returns \emph{undecided} on all three.

\paragraph{Misuse and limitations.} Reading \notest{} as ``no effect'' can turn
an undefined group-resolved estimand into a treatment-relevant mechanism claim.
Our calibration covers one gene, axis and weighting; its two cohorts disagree,
the draw pool was unspecified, and repeated holdouts are not independent. This
shows this rule failing its own criteria, not cell-count rules failing generally.

\paragraph{Impact and release.} Systems reporting cell-type-resolved quantities
need an abstention state, but calibrating that state is essential. The cohorts
are public, de-identified and previously published; because single-cell
genotypes can remain re-identifiable, we release code and derived summaries,
not cell-level matrices.
