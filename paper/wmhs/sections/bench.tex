We measured what alternatives do where the estimand does not exist, on synthetic
cells whose truth is available in closed form (Appendix~\ref{app:bench}). Mature
cells draw Poisson; immature cells express nothing; one world sets the diseased
mature fraction to zero.

Over 200 replicates in the world where no diseased mature cell survives, our
count gate and a width gate each refuse all 200, and three ungated methods ---
the same arithmetic with the gate removed, pseudobulk differential expression,
and a naive difference of arm means --- return a number every time
(Table~\ref{tab:bench}).

Ungated methods invent large effects. On a scale where the reference per-cell
mean is 20.0, the ungated ablation and pseudobulk differential expression each
invent a median $|$intrinsic$|$ of 7.99, and a naive difference of arm means
reports the entire 20.0-point drop as silencing in cells that are not there. We
include that last one to fix the scale, not as a serious competitor: nobody
proposes it as an intrinsic-effect estimator. Composition-only methods score no
false confidence for a different reason again --- they have no intrinsic arm at
all, which is inapplicability rather than caution.

\paragraph{The result that went against us.} The fair competitor is the width
gate, and it does the job as well as ours for free. It suppresses false
confidence completely, like our count gate, but costs no detections. The width
criterion never binds: in \texttt{depleted\_wide} the widest interval over 200
replicates is 1.081 against a detectable effect of 3.967, a factor of 3.7 clear.
So the width gate reduces to its \emph{finiteness guard} --- and that guard
alone is enough. \textbf{On this benchmark the only part of any gate that does
work is the check that the estimand exists at all.} Our count cutpoint's extra
88 refusals buy nothing measurable. Its slightly lower median error is a
selection effect, not an accuracy gain: it drops the noisy low-$n$ cases.
\S\ref{sec:calibration} found the same cutpoint unsupported by a second and
independent route.

\paragraph{The finiteness guard is one line, and easy to omit.} With no mature
cell in an arm the standard error is undefined, and \texttt{nan > threshold} is
\texttt{False} in numpy and in pandas. A width gate written the obvious way
therefore \emph{declines to abstain} in exactly the world the estimand does not
exist. We check finiteness before comparing, which makes this competitor
stronger than the version most people would write. It is also the coercion that
was sitting inside our own calibration routine (\S\ref{sec:calibration}), where
it arrived as a boolean in a rate instead of a number in a column.

\paragraph{Why a count cutpoint at all, rather than conformal prediction?}
Conformal methods give distribution-free coverage for a target that exists but
is uncertain \citep{vovk2005,angelopoulos2023}. The failure here is prior to
that: the parameter has no referent, so there is nothing to cover, and an
exchangeability argument over calibration data cannot manufacture one. Existence
and uncertainty are different questions, and the results above suggest we
answered the first one with a tool sized for the second. Conformal intervals are
the right instrument for the \emph{wide} verdict, where the estimand exists and
the interval is the whole question. We do not use them there yet.

\paragraph{A real-data instance, and the negative control it comes with.}
Everything above is synthetic. The same question --- what is this statistic a
function of? --- has a closed-form answer on the estimand itself. Writing
$\Delta f = f_T - f_N$, the ratio of Equation~\ref{eq:kitagawa}'s first two
terms is
\[
\frac{\text{intrinsic}}{\text{compositional}}
\;=\; \frac{f_N}{\Delta f}\left(\frac{m_T}{m_N} - 1\right),
\]
so as a group's surviving per-cell mean goes to zero the bracket tends to $-1$
and the ratio tends to $-f_N/\Delta f$ --- a quantity with no group-specific
term in it at all. Both terms are losses here, so the ratio is positive. On our carcinoma cohort at the \texttt{lineage} resolution
that limit is $6.94$, and the six panel genes whose surviving mean is nearest
zero --- $m_T/m_N$ from $0.045$ to $0.109$, a 2.4-fold range in their own
inputs --- return ratios from $6.19$ to $6.63$, a spread of $1.07$-fold. The
gene has cancelled, much as the estimator cancelled in \S\ref{sec:blind}, and
what survives is a property of the composition.

\textbf{The negative control is the same code on a different substrate.} On a
pre-malignant cohort no gene's surviving mean is near zero (the minimum
$m_T/m_N$ is $0.374$), and the identical computation returns $0.27$ to
$3.13$: an $11.5$-fold spread where carcinoma gave $1.07$. The degeneracy is
therefore a property of the substrate rather than of the estimator, and it is
legible \emph{before} any estimate is quoted. It also answers the objection
that abstention is trivially achievable by abstaining always --- on real data
this pipeline declined where the estimand degenerates and separated the genes
where it does not. We draw no biological conclusion from either cohort
(\S\ref{sec:responsible}); the claim here is about identifiability.


\paragraph{The finiteness result does not depend on the count model, and we can
prove that rather than hope it.} Appendix~\ref{app:bench} limitation~1 said this
benchmark tests the gate and not robustness to a misspecified generator. For the
finiteness guard specifically that limitation is now closed. Re-running
\texttt{annihilated} under six expression models --- Poisson, negative binomial
at dispersion $10$, $2$ and $0.5$, zero-inflated NB at $\pi = 0.3$ and
dispersion $2$, and zero-inflated Poisson at $\pi = 0.3$ --- over three seeds
at 200 replicates each, the count gate and the width gate each refuse $200/200$
in every model while the ungated ablation answers $200/200$ in every model.
There is an analytic reason and it is why this is a result: the world sets the
mature fraction to \emph{exactly} zero, so no mature cell exists whatever the
count distribution, and no amount of overdispersion or zero-inflation can
manufacture one. \textbf{It does not extend to the width gate.} In
\texttt{depleted\_wide} the count gate refuses 90 to 99 of 200 in every model,
averaging over the three seeds, and 83 to 105 across the eighteen
model-by-seed cells --- a spread the expression model barely widens. The width
gate refuses \emph{none} of 200 under Poisson, NB at $10$ and $2$, and
zero-inflated Poisson; one under zero-inflated NB; and fourteen under NB at
dispersion $0.5$. Its non-binding is a property of the expression model, and
the finiteness guard's behaviour is not.

\paragraph{And the width gate matches ours on a knife edge.} Sweeping the
detectable effect locates where the width criterion starts to bind, and the
answer is sharper than ``it never does''. There \emph{is} a setting reproducing
our count gate's 88 refusals in \texttt{depleted\_wide} --- but only for
$s_{\text{detect}} \in [0.928776, 0.928981]$, \textbf{a window $0.000204$
wide}. And $0.029$ above it, at $s_{\text{detect}} = 0.958$, the gate starts
refusing in \texttt{depleted\_estimable}, where the estimand plainly exists,
with \texttt{compositional\_only} following at $0.961$. The whole interval in
which it refuses everything in \texttt{depleted\_wide} and nothing it should
not is $0.0027$ wide. So the width gate does not dominate ours for free. It matches ours in a parameter nobody calibrated, with a cliff just past
it --- which is this paper's thesis arriving in our own preferred competitor.

\paragraph{Where this leaves the rule.} Two independent routes now say the same
thing about the count cutpoint, and neither says what we expected. The benchmark
says its extra refusals buy nothing a finiteness check does not already buy;
the calibration says the value its own criteria return is 90, not the committed
50. The
three-verdict output survives both --- as a design, though not at every seed,
since one of thirteen returns coincident cutpoints and no middle verdict at all.
What does not survive is the committed number, and the belief that either a
recovery curve or a coarse grid had confirmed it.
