\section{What the blind curve hid}
\label{sec:calibration}

Three acceptance criteria were fixed in a design document before any sweep ran:
a detectable halving of per-cell output, $s = 0.5$; interval coverage of 90\%;
and discrimination of 80\%, the probability the interval excludes zero at that
effect.\iffull{} The cutpoints follow mechanically --- the smallest $n$ meeting
both criteria, and the smallest meeting coverage alone.\fi The pre-registration is
mechanical rather than documentary: the panel, the labelling axes and the
falsification rule are frozen in a configuration file
\iffull
with a \texttt{frozen\_on} stamp and mirrored by an exact-value assertion in the
test suite, so continuous integration fails if anyone edits one without the
other.
\else
and asserted in the test suite, so continuous integration fails if anyone edits
one without the other.
\fi

Run properly on real held-out cells, the committed cutpoint of 50 does not come
back\iffull{} (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration})\fi. The generator filters eligibility on
patient alone, so both synthetic arms draw from the held-out patients' cells of
\emph{both} tissues pooled.
\iffull
The design document lists what stays fixed and never
mentions tissue. Nobody made that choice; an implementation detail made it, and
it changes the answer.
\else
The design document never mentions tissue: nobody made
that choice, an implementation detail made it, and it changes the answer.
\fi
So we report both readings rather than the one that
yields a cutpoint. Pooled, the routine returns no cutpoint at all: discrimination
never reaches the 0.80 target in any count bin, topping out at 0.78 across 13
seeds at 50 replicates and at 0.75 across 8 seeds at 500. Restricted to the
reference tissue it does return one, and that is where the second lesson of this
paper repeats itself.

\iffull\else
\paragraph{The grid was reporting its own resolution.} The committed grid varies
a mature \emph{fraction} against a fixed 2{,}000 cells, so it can reach no count
between 40 and 100, and returns $ok = 100$ on every seed. Adding nine fractions
inside that gap and changing nothing else moves the answer to 90, against a
committed 50: the crossing it was hiding runs $0.728$ at 45 mature cells,
$0.789$ at 65 and $0.858$ at 90. A grid that cannot resolve a crossing still
returns a stable number, which is \S\ref{sec:blind}'s failure in a second
statistic.
\fi
\iffull
\paragraph{The grid was reporting its own resolution.} Our committed grid varies
a mature \emph{fraction} against a fixed 2{,}000 cells, so the counts it can
reach are $\{0, 20, 40, 100, 200, 400, 800\}$ --- nothing between 40 and 100. On
the reference pool it returns $ok = 100$ on every one of the eight seeds at 500
replicates\iffull{}, and on twelve of thirteen at 50,\fi{} and that stability is
what made the number look like a measurement.

\iffull
The superseded internal report gave 120, and we cannot reproduce it: 120 is not
a count the committed grid can reach. Whatever produced it was not the committed
configuration, and the run is the uncommitted one of
Appendix~\ref{app:withdrawn} item~3, so there is nothing left to re-derive it
from. We report that rather than reconcile it.
\fi

We added nine
fractions inside the gap, reaching 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, 110, 120, 130 and 150,
and changed nothing else. \textbf{The answer moves to 90}, and the crossing it
was hiding becomes legible: median discrimination runs $0.728$ at 45 mature
cells, $0.789$ at 65 and $0.858$ at 90, so the pre-registered target of $0.80$
falls between 65 and 90 --- inside the interval the committed grid could not
reach. Seven of eight seeds return $ok = 90$, $wide = 45$; the eighth disagrees
and returns 65\iffull{}, which is the count where discrimination sits at $0.789$
against a $0.80$ target --- close enough to the line that a seed can land either
side of it\fi{}. Against committed values of 50 and 20, the calibrated rule is
roughly twice as strict on both cutpoints. The pooled draw returns no cutpoint
under either grid.
\fi

\iffull
\paragraph{And 90 is not a cutpoint with an interval on it.} At 50 replicates
the crossing does not resolve at all --- the returned value wanders between 45
and 90 with the seed. At 500 it does, 7 of 8 seeds returning $ok = 90$, but that
stabilises Monte Carlo noise and not the quantity a reader will take it for. Ten
patients with two held out per replicate admit just \textbf{45 distinct holdout
pairs}, so at 500 replicates each pair is drawn about eleven times and the
estimate converges to the value over those 45 pairs rather than to a population
value. The patient-level uncertainty is unchanged by the replicate count and is
not quantified anywhere in this paper. Read $ok = 90$ as what this procedure
returns on this cohort. Reporting it with an interval would be the same error
this paper documents twice elsewhere, one section later.
\fi

\iffull
\paragraph{A second cohort returns a different number, and the same verdict.}
The two Lee cohorts are both local and both sha256-verified, and only one had
ever been run. On the second --- six patients against ten --- the pooled draw
again returns \emph{no} cutpoint, on every seed at both replicate counts: no
seed meets both criteria, and discrimination alone reaches the $0.80$ target at
50 replicates but tops out at $0.66$ at 500. The reference draw returns a
cutpoint, and it is nowhere near the committed 50: $ok = 400$ or $800$ on both
grids, the top two counts either can express, against $90$ on the first cohort's
filled grid. \textbf{The pre-registered procedure returns a cutpoint roughly twice
the committed one on one cohort and eight to sixteen times it on the other},
which is a statement about the procedure rather than about either cohort. The
blindness, being algebraic, replicates exactly: the residual is $0$ on all
$1{,}711{,}200$ replicates across both.

The instability is worse than the first cohort's, not better. At 50 replicates
the second cohort returns $ok = 800$ on eleven of thirteen seeds; at 500 it
returns $400$ on six of eight. \textbf{The majority answer moved when we added
replicates}, which is what Appendix~\ref{app:limitations} predicts --- more
replicates buy Monte Carlo precision, not patient-level precision, and six
patients admit fifteen distinct holdout pairs against the first cohort's
forty-five. The top grid point also asks for 800 mature cells per arm from a
pool holding 844, so that end of the grid is at the edge of what the cohort can
express in distinct cells; we report the number and the edge together.
\fi
