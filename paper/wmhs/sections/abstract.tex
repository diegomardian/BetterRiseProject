\begin{abstract}
Recovery curves compare estimated effects with a simulator's requested truth.
They can conceal that an estimator reproduces a reference statistic of the draw.
If both compute the same functional of the realised summaries, their difference
is identically zero and recovery reduces to realised over requested truth.
This equality does not invalidate sampling-performance evaluation; it prevents
that curve alone from establishing additional calibration claims.
We propose an audit column: compare the estimate with a specified realised
reference and investigate a uniformly zero residual. Our single-cell oracle
arm reproduced its reference across 1.7M runs, yet the abstention rule its curve
had certified did not survive recalibration. A patient simulator demonstrates
the same equality for saturated G-computation, IPW and non-cross-fitted AIPW.
Cross-fitted IPW breaks it in the tested design; exact balance within strata
makes even the unadjusted difference reproduce the reference.
\iffull
A residual-to-generator-error ratio quantifies departures from equality without
attributing fractions of total error. Under censoring, the exponential MLE
reproduces the observed-data reference but differs from the latent-time
reference. These comparisons diagnose functional reuse, not estimator quality.
\else
The audit complements interval coverage and decision-rule calibration; it does
not certify either.
\fi
\end{abstract}
