\paragraph{Scope and interpretation.}
This paper makes methods and validation claims, not biological or clinical
claims. The pre-committed control panel included genes expected to change
compositionally, genes expected to change intrinsically, and a retained-gene
control. In the primary cohort, the retained-gene control changed by
$-1.000$ to $-0.951$ relative to baseline, almost matching the compositional
controls' $-1.000$ to $-0.952$ range. The intrinsic control was too weakly
expressed to provide a useful test (0.04 CP10K versus 19.4 and 24.9 for the two
abundant compositional controls; CP10K denotes counts per 10,000).
The controls therefore did not establish the intended mechanistic separation.
A second cohort reproduced the pattern, without resolving its interpretation.

\paragraph{Misuse and limitations.}
\notest{} must not be read as ``no effect'' or as evidence that surviving cells
are behaving normally. A separate within-cell analysis over three cohorts and
42 patients also remained unresolved because its comparability checks returned
\emph{undecided} (Appendix~\ref{app:guards}). Calibration here covers one gene,
one labelling axis and one weighting on two small cohorts. It challenges this
rule's justification, not cell-count rules in general. The draw-pool choice was
underspecified, repeated simulations do not add independent patients, and
patient-level uncertainty in the candidate cutpoints remains unquantified.
The absence-defined labelling axis remains sensitive to sequencing depth
(Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}). The patient simulations illustrate possible
functional equalities; they do not estimate their prevalence in deployed
virtual control arms.

\paragraph{Data and impact.}
The single-cell cohorts are public, de-identified and previously published.
The release policy covers derived summaries and code, rather than cell-level
matrices. A system reporting group-specific quantities should distinguish an
unavailable comparison from an estimated null and should evaluate the coverage
and discrimination of any abstention rule. Publishing failed calibration
attempts helps prevent unsupported thresholds from being treated as validated.
