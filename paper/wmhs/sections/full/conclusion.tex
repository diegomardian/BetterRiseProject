A recovery curve can describe sampling performance while adding no comparison
beyond a specified empirical reference. Reporting the residual against that
reference makes this limitation visible. In our patient simulator, equality
depends on the estimator, reference functional and treatment-assignment design;
under censoring, observed-data and latent-time references answer different
questions. Neither equality nor departure ranks estimator quality.

The motivating calibration shows why the distinction matters. The recovery
curve was used to justify a cell-count rule whose direct coverage and
discrimination evaluation returned either no qualifying cutpoint or candidates
that depended on the grid and cohort. A separate synthetic benchmark
(Appendix~\ref{app:bench}) found that checking for an undefined comparison
prevented the same false interpretations as the count gate, with fewer refusals
under the tested Poisson model.

Pre-specify the reference, report non-finite outputs and residual scale, and
investigate numerical equality algebraically. Continue to evaluate error
against the requested parameter, interval coverage and the decision rule
separately. These checks establish different properties; a convincing recovery
curve cannot substitute for all of them.
