\section{Supporting simulation results}
\label{app:generator}

\paragraph{Draw-pool sensitivity and the null case.}
The sweep in \texttt{src.harness.calibration\_gap} stores a zero maximum
residual on every row. At $f_T=0.01$ (about 20
mature cells), $s=0.5$ and 500 replicates, median recovery across eight seeds
ranges from $1.331$ to $1.423$ for pooled draws and $1.075$ to $1.108$ for
reference draws. The IQR width increases from about $0.62$ to $1.17$, while
the residual remains zero. At the abstention point, the recovery ratio has
closed form $1/(1-s)$, giving $5.000$, $2.000$ and $1.333$ at the three tested
shifts with zero replicate variance. At $s=1$, the requested intrinsic term is
zero and recovery ratios are undefined; differences remain available.

Regularisation also changes the residual. For logistic-propensity IPW in the
confounded design, maximum residuals against the standardised reference are
$0.403$, $0.045$, $0.0103$ and $0.010$ at $C=0.1,1,100,10^6$; tightening
solver tolerance to $10^{-8}$ reduces the final value to $1.3\times10^{-6}$.
Both nuisance-model fitting and numerical convergence affect an equality screen.

Under increasing effect heterogeneity ($\tau=0$ to 16, holding the population
ATE fixed), OLS recovery ratios are $1.003$, $1.011$, $1.020$, $1.037$,
$1.077$ and $1.132$. Its maximum standardised-reference residual grows from
$0.094$ to $1.452$, while its own variance-weighted-reference residual remains
between $2.4\times10^{-14}$ and $9.7\times10^{-14}$. The recovery curve
correctly detects a target mismatch: under heterogeneity, OLS's
variance-weighted population target can differ from the requested ATE.
Likewise, increasing propensity differences from randomised toward confounded
assignment raises OLS's standardised-reference residual from $0.0033$ to
$0.104$ while recovery remains approximately 1.00. Residual size alone cannot
rank either calibration or accuracy.

\section{Calibration and real-data caveats}
\label{app:gridcaveats}

\paragraph{Denominators and coincident cutpoints.}
Undefined intervals were previously counted as coverage and discrimination
failures because comparisons with \texttt{NaN} evaluated to false.
The corrected routine reports abstentions separately and conditions interval
rates on defined outputs; an all-abstaining bin has no coverage estimate.
At one of thirteen seeds in the 50-replicate filled-grid sweep, $ok=wide=45$,
so the fitted rule has no middle state. Calibrating a three-state rule does
not guarantee three distinct output regions.

\subsection{Abstention and annotation resolution}
\label{app:modal}
At the finest annotation resolution, $28/28$ patients abstain under one
pre-registered population definition and $24/28$ under the other. Both exceed
the pre-committed $50\%$ limit. At coarser resolutions, all six combinations
remain \emph{not identifiable}: two because the population definitions disagree,
and four because at least one arm is unresolved.

The coarsest resolution labels every epithelial cell mature, forcing the
compositional term to zero on all 1{,}728 rows. At the finest resolution,
1{,}080 of 1{,}296 rows are \notest{}, none receives an unqualified estimate,
and the median mature-cell count is three. The middle two resolutions assign
the same mature sets on all 30 patients of the primary axis: Jaccard similarity
is 1.000 for 29 and undefined for the patient with two empty sets.

\subsection{Diagnostic reachability and interval calibration}
\label{app:guards}

\paragraph{Saturated controls can miss expression changes.}
An exploratory within-cell analysis used housekeeping-gene detection to assess
comparability between tissues. Across three cohorts and 42 patients, 83 of 84
control rows fell within a 0.10 tolerance despite output changes of 1.19--1.64
fold. Detection near one limits sensitivity: under Poisson thinning,
$d'=1-(1-d)^s$. Patient-bootstrap log$_2$ expression intervals instead crossed
the accepted range's boundaries, $[-0.5,0.5]$, in every cohort, leaving all
three comparisons \emph{undecided}.

\paragraph{Small-sample bootstrap intervals need separate calibration.}
For a mean of $n$ observations with sample standard deviation $s$, the
conditional bootstrap standard deviation is $s\sqrt{(n-1)/n}/\sqrt n$.
A normal approximation to a percentile interval therefore has width, relative
to a normal-population Student-$t$ interval,
\[
\frac{z\sqrt{(n-1)/n}}{t_{n-1}}.
\]
This approximation applies to a mean, not arbitrary percentile intervals.
At $n=10$ the width ratio is $0.82$, with an implied normal-population
rejection rate of $9.6\%$ for a nominal $5\%$ test. In the twenty-cell
sweep (1{,}500 trials per cell), percentile intervals are miscalibrated in 14
cells and BCa in 17 under the sweep's criterion. Student-$t$ intervals are
calibrated in 19 and conservative in one, with worst rejection rate $5.9\%$.
These results concern the tested settings and do not establish that one
interval method is universally preferable. More resamples reduce simulation
noise, not uncertainty from a small patient sample.

\section{Synthetic abstention benchmark}
\label{app:bench}
\label{app:benchtable}

The benchmark uses six synthetic worlds, 200 replicates per world, 2{,}000
cells per arm and seed 20260829. Mature-cell expression is
$\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)$ in reference tissue and $\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu s)$ in
diseased tissue; immature cells express zero. The decomposition truth follows
from Equation~\ref{eq:kitagawa}. In \texttt{annihilated}, the diseased mature
fraction is exactly zero, so the diseased mature-cell mean has no referent.

The count gate is compared with identical arithmetic without gating and with
a width gate that abstains when the standard error is non-finite or the 95\%
half-width exceeds the pre-specified detectable effect. Check finiteness first:
\texttt{nan > threshold} is false and would otherwise permit an undefined
comparison. Five of six planned methods run; \texttt{cacoa} is unavailable in the pinned
environment. Competitors are reimplementations.

Both gates refuse all 200 annihilated replicates. The ungated ablation,
pseudobulk differential expression and a naive arm-mean difference return a
number in all 200. The latter two estimate different contrasts; their returned
values are not intrinsically erroneous, but cannot be interpreted as an
intrinsic effect in absent cells. The matched-arithmetic ablation establishes
the gate's effect without that estimand mismatch (Table~\ref{tab:bench}).
\input{sections/full/benchtable.tex}

\paragraph{Finiteness and width play different roles.}
Under Poisson expression, the width criterion never binds: in
\texttt{depleted\_wide}, the largest 95\% half-width is 1.081 versus a
detectable effect of 3.967. Its finiteness check prevents undefined outputs,
while the count gate makes 88 additional refusals without improving the
benchmark's false-confidence rate. The count gate's lower median error is
conditional on selecting fewer, less noisy cases.

The existence result persists under six expression models: Poisson, negative
binomial with dispersion 10, 2 or 0.5, zero-inflated negative binomial with
$\pi=0.3$ and dispersion 2, and zero-inflated Poisson with $\pi=0.3$.
Across three seeds and 200 replicates each, both gates refuse all annihilated
replicates in every model, while the ungated ablation returns a number in all.
This follows from setting the mature fraction to zero, independently of the
expression distribution. Width behaviour does change: in
\texttt{depleted\_wide}, mean refusals range from zero to fourteen across
these models, compared with 90--99 for the count gate (83--105 across individual
model-by-seed cells).

Changing the detectable effect also changes width-based refusals. Matching the
count gate's 88 refusals requires
$s_{\mathrm{detect}}\in[0.928776,0.928981]$ in this sweep. Refusals begin in
\texttt{depleted\_estimable} at $0.958$ and in \texttt{compositional\_only} at
$0.961$. Width-based refusals therefore depend on the model and threshold. Existence
and uncertainty checks require separate evaluation.

\section{Related work}
\label{app:relatedwork}
Simulation-study methodology separates the generator, estimand, methods and
performance measures \citep{burton2006,morris2019}. Our audit examines whether
a method equals a specified empirical reference. Cell-count filters are
established in pseudobulk analysis \citep{crowell2020}; scCODA, Milo, propeller
and DA-seq address composition \citep{buettner2021,dann2022,phipson2022,zhao2021},
while cacoa separates compositional and expression changes \citep{petukhov2022}.
The connection to selective prediction is the need to evaluate decisions to
answer or abstain \citep{chow1970,elyaniv2010,geifman2017}. Conformal coverage
for prediction targets \citep{vovk2005,angelopoulos2023} neither defines a missing
conditional population nor automatically supplies confidence intervals for the
intrinsic parameter here.

\section{Implementation corrections and provenance}
\label{app:withdrawn}
Corrections to the motivating analysis separated population definitions that
had been collapsed by de-duplication, derived fractions and means from a single
labelling call, and included untracked producing scripts in provenance checks.
The calibration was re-derived with the committed sweep. Regression fixtures
exercise mismatched identifiers, mispaired correlation bounds, undefined
interval denominators and untracked scripts. These implementation checks do
not establish the statistical validity of the resulting estimates.

\section{Extended limitations}
\label{app:limitations}
Neither draw pool reproduces the real tissue contrast: each supplies both
synthetic arms. They differ jointly in expression dispersion, distinct
mature-cell counts (662 versus 3{,}824, sampled with replacement) and tissue
mixture; the experiment does not isolate these contributions.

Depth matching discards roughly three quarters of the cells and moves about
half the remaining estimable rows below the count threshold. Estimates are
calculated per study, but only one study-by-resolution comparison supports the
intended summary after matching, so multi-study synthesis is unavailable.
Real-data inference resamples patients, not cells, and remains subject to the
small-sample limitations in Appendix~\ref{app:guards}.

Keep abstention counts, conditional interval performance and the interaction
term explicit downstream. Storing \texttt{None} prevents substitution with zero
but cannot prevent an undefined interval from entering a rate denominator.
The benchmark supports conclusions about the tested gate implementations and
regimes, not general robustness or clinical utility.
