\paragraph{On real data, abstention is the modal outcome.} At the finest
annotation resolution, the one the compositional question is defined on, $28/28$
patients abstain under one pre-registered population definition and $24/28$
under the other. Both fail the pre-committed 50\% line. The specification
produces this, not the data: 5\% of a few hundred cells falls below a cutpoint of
20 by construction, so the resolution is defined at a granularity its own cohort
cannot support. At coarser resolutions the two definitions disagree and the
pre-registered response is \emph{not identifiable} rather than a choice --- all
six coarse combinations return that verdict, where a method forced to pick one
definition returns six confident numbers.

\paragraph{And the resolution sweep has one usable point.} The design promised a
curve across four annotation resolutions. The coarsest is estimable and vacuous:
every epithelial cell is called mature, so $f_T - f_N = 0$ and the compositional
term is exactly $0.0$ on all $1{,}728$ rows. The finest is the reverse ---
$1{,}080$ of $1{,}296$ rows are \notest{}, none reaches the unqualified
\emph{estimate} verdict, and the median mature-cell count is $3$. The two in
between are the same partition: on the primary labelling axis they assign
identical mature sets on all $30$ patients. Jaccard is $1.000$ on 29 of them and
\emph{undefined} on the thirtieth, where both rungs assign zero mature cells and
the index is $0/0$ --- a patient that a mean over that column would drop without
saying so. \textbf{A four-point curve with one real point still plots as a
curve}, which is the shape of \S\ref{sec:blind} in a third statistic --- and the
only one of the three that is not a simulation.
