\begin{abstract}
Simulate with known truth, run the estimator, plot recovered against true: this
is how virtual control arms and in-silico trial pipelines are validated. We show
the curve can be structurally incapable of seeing the estimator. If the estimator
is a deterministic function of the sufficient statistics the generator drew, it
cancels, and the ratio becomes realised truth over requested truth --- a property
of the generator alone. The curve still sits near 1 and still tightens with $n$.
One line detects it: compare against the \emph{realised} truth and test whether
the difference is identically zero. Ours was, across 119{,}600 runs. In a patient
simulator this catches G-computation \emph{and} saturated-propensity weighting,
a different method whose curve is identical to machine precision, while
cross-fitting breaks the degeneracy and restores an informative curve. The
abstention rule our blind curve had certified does not survive recalibration.
\end{abstract}
