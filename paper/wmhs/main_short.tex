\begin{abstract}
Simulate with known truth, run the estimator, plot recovered against true: this
is how virtual control arms and in-silico trial pipelines are validated. We show
the curve can be structurally incapable of seeing the estimator. If the
estimator is a deterministic function of the sufficient statistics the generator
drew, it cancels identically and the ratio becomes realised over requested truth
--- a property of the generator alone, and the curve then looks not suspicious
but exactly right, near 1 and tightening with $n$. \textbf{A validation that
fails loudly is a nuisance; one that succeeds silently is a hazard.} One column
separates them: compare against the \emph{realised} draw and test whether the
difference is identically zero. Ours was, across 1.7M runs. In a patient
simulator it catches G-computation, saturated-propensity weighting and
non-cross-fitted AIPW --- so cross-fitting is what stands between the modern
doubly-robust estimator and a vacuous curve --- while under the block
randomisation real trials use, even an unadjusted difference goes blind. The
appendix carries what the check still cannot see, what censoring does to it, and
the recalibration that followed.
\end{abstract}
