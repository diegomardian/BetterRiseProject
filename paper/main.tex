\begin{abstract}
A marker vanishes from a diseased tissue: did the cells making it leave, or did
the survivors turn it down? Single-cell data promises the answer bulk cannot
give. We built that measurement for colorectal cancer behind four guards --- a
pre-registered control panel, a confound diagnostic, a simulation-based
validation sweep, and an abstention rule --- and all four passed while measuring
nothing. The failures are structural rather than coding errors, and each one is
invisible to the guard built to catch it. Our sharpest result is a bound: a
correlation-threshold check against a binary label of prevalence $p$ cannot
exceed $\sqrt{3p(1-p)}$, so on rare cell types the check is arithmetically
incapable of firing and reports \emph{clean}. The blind spot sits exactly on the
populations worth resolving. We show three further cases --- a control panel that
cannot separate its tiers for algebraic reasons and whose failure
\emph{replicates} across cohorts, a validation curve in which the estimator
cancels exactly so the curve measures the simulator, and an abstention rule whose
own calibration scores refusals as failures. We also report where these failures
\emph{stop}: the same depth mechanism, tested on bulk data from the same disease
with the arms unbalanced in the same way, does not transfer. We give the
one-line diagnostics that would have caught each case, and one instance of
catching a fifth prospectively.
\end{abstract}

\section{The measurement, and the trap underneath it}
\label{sec:intro}

A differentiation marker falls 90\% between a patient's healthy and diseased
tissue. Two mechanisms produce the drop and they point to different
interventions. The cells making the marker have gone, a \emph{compositional}
change. Or those cells remain and produce less, an \emph{intrinsic} change. Bulk
expression cannot separate the two. Single-cell data separates them by counting
cells of the type in question in each arm and averaging their output. Write $f$
for the fraction of such cells and $m$ for their mean per-cell output. Kitagawa's
demographic standardisation \citep{kitagawa1955} splits the observed contrast:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{(f_T - f_N)\, m_N}_{\text{compositional}} \;+\;
\underbrace{f_N\, (m_T - m_N)}_{\text{intrinsic}} \;+\;
\underbrace{(f_T - f_N)(m_T - m_N)}_{\text{interaction}}
\;=\; f_T m_T - f_N m_N .
\label{eq:kitagawa}
\end{equation}
Economists call the identity Oaxaca--Blinder \citep{oaxaca1973,blinder1973},
epidemiologists call it standardisation \citep{kline2011}. Nothing here
criticises the estimator, which is arithmetic.

\paragraph{The trap.} Equation~\ref{eq:kitagawa} needs $f$, and $f$ needs a
label: which cells belong to the type in question. Labelling needs marker genes.
The markers of the mature cell type \emph{are} the genes whose expression the
measurement averages, since $m$ is their mean. Using them as labels makes a
silenced cell indistinguishable from an absent one, the exact confusion the study
exists to resolve, so a leakage invariant bars them.

What remains is defining the state by the \emph{absence} of markers of the
opposite state. Everything in \S\ref{sec:depth} follows from that one forced
move, and the move is forced for any panel where a state's positive markers
double as the quantity of interest.

\paragraph{What is and is not new here.} That sequencing depth confounds
single-cell labels is known; dropout is the founding problem of the field
\citep{kharchenko2014}, and compositional inference
\citep{buttner2021sccoda,dann2022milo} and deconvolution
\citep{newman2019cibersortx} each carry their own literature on it. Confounded
analyses surviving standard controls is also documented
\citep{squair2021confronting}. \textbf{Our contribution is not the confound. It
is that the standard guards against it are structurally unable to detect it},
and that this is demonstrable from the guard's own algebra before any data is
collected.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A closed-form blindness bound} (\S\ref{sec:ceiling}). A correlation
  check against a binary label of prevalence $p$ is bounded by
  $\sqrt{3p(1-p)}$. Any tolerance above that bound is a check that cannot fail,
  and rare populations put it there. On our data the check never fires on 32 of
  48 patient-by-resolution combinations and reports the rarest resolution as the
  cleanest of four.
\item \textbf{A boundary condition} (\S\ref{sec:boundary}). The same mechanism
  tested on bulk data from the same disease, with the arms unbalanced in the same
  way, does \emph{not} transfer. The failure mode is specific to absence-defined
  labels at single-cell depth, not to marker-based measurement generally.
\item \textbf{Two further structural failures} (\S\ref{sec:controls},
  \S\ref{sec:oracle}): a control panel that cannot separate its own tiers for
  reasons available from the estimator's algebra, whose non-separation
  \emph{replicates} across cohorts; and a validation curve in which the estimator
  cancels exactly, leaving a property of the simulator.
\item \textbf{Diagnostics.} A one-line check for each, and one case
  (\S\ref{sec:takeaways}) where the same class of defect was caught
  \emph{before} the analysis ran rather than after.
\end{itemize}

Our test throughout: \emph{would a different group, writing correct code, still
hit this?} Five defects from our own history fail that test and sit in
Appendix~\ref{app:ours} as record rather than finding.

\section{The label reads the instrument}
\label{sec:depth}

A cell gets called mature when no marker of the stem-like state appears in it.
Whether a marker appears depends on how deeply the cell was sequenced. The label
is therefore a function of the instrument as well as the biology.

On the replication cohort the maturity call correlates with library size at
$\rho = +0.33$ against an attainable bound of $0.52$ for a label of its
prevalence --- 63\% of the largest value the statistic can take,
\emph{after} the correction below. Following \S\ref{sec:ceiling}, we state the
pairing explicitly: \textbf{both the correlation and the bound are computed on
the depleted arm}, from that arm's own prevalence. Reporting a
maximum-over-arms correlation against the other arm's bound is the error
\S\ref{sec:ceiling} describes us making three times, and it is the reason this
sentence names the arm. Normalising to counts per 10{,}000 changes nothing,
because scaling never undoes a zero.

\paragraph{Why the label has to look like this.} The design did not pick this
over an alternative. The markers of the mature state are the genes the intrinsic
term averages, so the leakage invariant bars them from the label, and what
remains is the absence of markers of the opposite state. Any panel where a
state's positive markers double as the quantity of interest lands in the same
place, with a label reading dropout.

\paragraph{The correction, and what it costs.} Thinning each cell's marker counts
to a common depth before scoring, then refusing to score cells below the floor,
removes the mechanism instead of adjusting for it. A cell too shallow to have
shown a marker gets marked unscored, never immature. That is an abstention
verdict applied to a \emph{label}, on the same grounds as the one applied to an
estimate in \S\ref{sec:abstention}. The two arms stay incomparable at 2.36$\times$
apart in median depth, so we additionally equalise their depth distributions by
construction, keeping $\min(n_N, n_T)$ cells per depth bin. The equalisation
costs three quarters of the cells, and the surviving rows shift from 36/0/4 to
16/16/8 across \emph{estimate}, \emph{wide} and \emph{abstain}. \textbf{On this
cohort you get a clean comparison or an estimable one, never both.} The trade
belongs to the correction, not to the cohort size.

An earlier internal figure put this artefact far larger, in the hypothesised
direction. We could not re-derive it and have withdrawn it; the episode and what
it cost are in Appendix~\ref{app:withdrawn}, and the lesson is
\S\ref{sec:takeaways}'s last.

\section{Where the failure stops}
\label{sec:boundary}

A failure mode that appears everywhere we looked is hard to distinguish from a
pipeline that never had signal. So we tested the same mechanism where it should
\emph{not} apply: bulk RNA-seq from the same disease, $n = 624$ tumours and 51
matched normals, where the marker's level is measured directly rather than
inferred from a label.

\textbf{The precondition holds.} The arms are unbalanced in depth exactly as in
\S\ref{sec:depth} --- median library size 36.5M in the diseased arm against 48.4M
in the healthy one, $p = 5 \times 10^{-10}$. Note the sign is \emph{reversed}
from the single-cell cohort, where the healthy arm is the shallower one.

\textbf{The mechanism does not transfer.} Non-detection of the marker is flat
across library-size deciles at $\leq 1.6\%$ per decile, against the
80.8\%~$\rightarrow$~8.5\% monotone gradient the single-cell cohort shows. Library
size explains $r^2 = 0.046$ of the marker's variance, and the association runs
the wrong way for a dropout artefact: deeper libraries carry \emph{less} of the
marker, where dropout would make shallow samples read low. Conditioning on
library size moves the distributional test that this arm's conclusion rests on
from $p = 0.982$ to $p = 0.992$.

\textbf{What this buys.} The failure in \S\ref{sec:depth} is not a property of
marker-based measurement in general, nor of this disease, nor of these genes. It
is a property of \emph{labels defined by absence, at depths where absence is
mostly sampling}. At $\sim$36M reads there is no dropout to mistake for signal;
at $\sim$5{,}000 counts per cell there is little else. Stating where a failure
mode stops is what makes it a finding rather than an anecdote, and it is the
control we most nearly failed to run.

\section{The confound check cannot fire where the science is}
\label{sec:ceiling}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_ceiling.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{A correlation-threshold confound check goes structurally blind
on rare cell types.} \emph{Left:} the attainable bound $\sqrt{3p(1-p)}$ against
the prevalence of the labelled state, with every patient-by-resolution
measurement plotted at its own prevalence and correlation, in the depleted arm.
Below $p = 1.37\%$ the 0.20 tolerance sits above the bound and the check never
fires. \emph{Right:} each correlation as a share of its own bound. The three
resolutions look alike on the reference arm at 0.17 to 0.18. On the depleted arm
the rarest runs at twice the others, the opposite of what the raw statistic
reports.}
\label{fig:ceiling}
\end{figure}

The obvious guard against \S\ref{sec:depth} correlates the maturity call against
sequencing depth and flags anything above a tolerance. Ours sat at
$|\rho| \geq 0.20$. The guard never fired on the rarest annotation resolution,
and a results sidecar recorded that resolution as the cleanest of four.

The guard was never going to fire. The correlation between a continuous variable
and a \emph{binary} label of prevalence $p$ carries a bound of
$\sqrt{3p(1-p)}$, which we verified numerically against perfect separation. Below
$p = 1.37\%$ a tolerance of 0.20 sits above the bound. On one cohort the rarest
resolution held 8 positive cells in 5{,}564, a bound of 0.066, and the check
returned ``clean, $|\rho| = 0.055$''. On the primary cohort the depleted arm's
prevalence at that resolution runs 0.6\% with a bound of 0.137, \textbf{under the
tolerance on 32 of 48 patient-by-resolution combinations}.

Normalising by the attainable bound inverts the ranking
(Figure~\ref{fig:ceiling}, right). The rarest resolution sits at 0.185 of its
bound on the depleted arm against 0.092 and 0.094 for the two coarser ones.
\textbf{The blind spot sits exactly where the science is.} Rare populations are
the ones worth resolving, and the ones this class of check never flags.

\paragraph{Our repair inherited the defect.} We fixed the diagnostic to report
the attainable bound alongside the statistic. The fix took the worst correlation
and the largest bound as \emph{independent} maxima over the two arms, so the
statistic and its bound sometimes came from different arms. Correcting the
pairing moves 66 of 190 patient-by-resolution rows, by up to 3.9$\times$, and
flips the reachability flag at the rarest resolution from ``capable of firing''
on \textbf{64 of 64} combinations down to 16.

We then made the same error a third time, inside the analysis of the fix.
Normalising the shipped table after the fact requires pairing a
maximum-over-arms correlation with one arm's prevalence, and 21 of 166 rows then
sit \emph{above their own bound}, 8 of them where that arm's prevalence is zero.
The violation appears the moment you plot the points against the bound, and stays
invisible in a table. The diagnostic now persists both arms' correlation, bound,
prevalence and depth, so nobody downstream repeats the pairing --- and
\S\ref{sec:depth}'s headline number is reported under that discipline.

\section{The control panel cannot separate, and it replicates}
\label{sec:controls}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_tiers.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The retained-gene control gets lost as hard as the compositional
controls.} Relative intrinsic change per gene, one point per stratum, on the
primary cohort. Strata cross two quality-control floors, two population
definitions, and matched against unmatched. Tier D was chosen because disease
retains the gene, which should leave both arms alone. Tier D lands inside tier
A's range. Tier B, the intrinsic control, changes sign between strata.}
\label{fig:tiers}
\end{figure}

Our pre-registered control panel names three tiers. Genes expected to move
because cells vanished (A). Genes expected to move because cells went quiet (B).
And one chosen because disease \emph{retains} it, which should leave both arms
alone (D). Tier D is the check deciding whether the decomposition measures what
it claims.

Across all eight strata of the primary cohort, the retained-gene control moves by
$-0.57$ to $-0.82$ relative to its own baseline, against $-0.62$ to $-1.11$ for
the compositional controls (Figure~\ref{fig:tiers}). The intrinsic control
changes \emph{sign} between strata. Tier D, the gene chosen because disease keeps
it, gets lost about as hard as the genes chosen because disease removes them.

\paragraph{Algebra produces this, not biology.} With
$\mathrm{comp} = \Delta f\, m_N$ and $\mathrm{intr} = f_N (m_T - m_N)$, the ratio
between the arms equals $(f_N/\Delta f)(m_T/m_N - 1)$. The gene enters only
through $m_T/m_N$, which sits near zero for \emph{any} strongly depleted gene.
Measured per patient, the ratios agree to three decimals across tiers and follow
from the cell fractions alone. This estimator was never going to separate the
tiers, at any sample size, for any genes with this depletion profile.

\paragraph{And the failure replicates.} A second cohort with different patients
shows the same pattern, agreeing to two decimal places on the retained control.
Replication across cohorts is the standard evidence a finding is not an artefact
of one dataset. Here it shows the artefact is not an artefact of one dataset.
\textbf{A structural failure replicates}, and a control panel reproducing its own
non-separation looks exactly like a control panel that works. The bulk arm sees
the tier-D gene lost $\sim$200-fold at the median as well --- recorded before the
decomposition existed --- so the panel's tier assignment, not the estimator
alone, is implicated.

\section{The validation curve cannot see the estimator}
\label{sec:oracle}

The standard way to validate an estimator like ours is simulation. Generate
samples with a known composition and a known shift, run the estimator, plot
recovered against true. Ours sat between 0.95 and 1.07 wherever the abstention
rule said ``estimate''. An internal report read the curve as the cutpoint being
validated on real cells.

The estimator in that arm applies Equation~\ref{eq:kitagawa} to the sample's
\emph{own} realised summary statistics, so it reproduces them exactly. Across all
4{,}400 runs, $\max|\hat{\imath} - \imath_{\text{realised}}| = 0$. The recovery
ratio therefore equals
$\imath_{\text{realised}} / \imath_{\text{parametric}}$, a comparison between
what the generator was asked for and what it drew, in which the estimator
cancels. Validating the arm with the ratio is circular.

The tell sat in the internal report and got read the wrong way. At a zero mature
fraction the ratio takes the value 2.004167, read at the time as the estimator
over-estimating two-fold in the direction reading as a stronger finding. The
value is $1/(1-s)$ scaled by the realised-over-requested mature fraction, a
closed form with \emph{zero variance} across all 50 replicates, taking the values
5.010417, 2.004167 and 1.336111 at the three shifts. A constant got read as a
measurement.

The statistic still catches an estimator failing to reproduce its input. The
design document ships a broken-estimator control returning $0.0$, and the ratio
comes out zero for it. The oracle arm by construction is not such an estimator,
and the design never says so.

\section{What we do instead, and what it costs}
\label{sec:abstention}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig1_calibration.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Calibrating the abstention rule returns 120 cells against a
committed 50, or nothing at all.} Coverage and discrimination against the cells
available to the intrinsic comparison. Dashed lines mark the targets fixed before
the sweep, shading marks the committed rule's three states. The two panels differ
only in a draw-pool choice the design document never makes.}
\label{fig:calibration}
\end{figure}

The response to all four failures is letting the estimator refuse. Our
decomposition returns one of three verdicts per patient, gene and resolution: an
estimate, a wide-interval estimate, or \notest{}, where the intrinsic term is
\texttt{None} and never $0.0$. Zero and ``we did not ask'' are the two values a
downstream average cannot tell apart. Abstention dominates where the
compositional question gets sharpest. At the finest resolution 28 of 28 patients
abstain under one population definition and 24 of 28 under the other.

\paragraph{The rule's own calibration fails.} Three acceptance criteria were
fixed before any sweep ran: a detectable halving of per-cell output, 90\%
interval coverage, and 80\% discrimination. Running the procedure on held-out
real cells for the first time, the committed cutpoint of 50 cells is not what
comes back (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}). Under the draw pool our sweep code
implements, no tested count meets both criteria and the routine returns nothing.
Under a second reading of the same unspecified choice, it returns 120.

\paragraph{And the calibration table scored refusals as failures.} Its lowest bin
merged the replicates holding no interval, the abstention case, with those
holding one. Comparisons against \texttt{NaN} return false, so undefined
intervals counted as coverage \emph{and} discrimination failures. The published
row read $(0.36, 0.31)$ where the defined replicates give $(0.72, 0.62)$, exactly
half. \textbf{An abstention silently counted as a failure, inside the routine
calibrating the rule built to prevent exactly this.} The coercion arrived as a
boolean in a rate rather than a number in a column, where the writer's assertion
never sees it.

\section{What to take from this}
\label{sec:takeaways}

\textbf{Report the attainable bound beside every threshold check.} A correlation
against a rare binary label never exceeds $\sqrt{3p(1-p)}$. A threshold above
that bound is a check unable to fail. Where the bound sits below the threshold,
the honest output is \emph{not testable} rather than \emph{clean}, an abstention
verdict for the check on the same grounds as one for an estimate.

\textbf{Ask whether a control panel could separate before you run it.} Ours could
not, for reasons available from the estimator's algebra alone, with no data. The
question costs an afternoon and we never asked.

\textbf{Ask what your validation statistic is a function of.} If your model is a
deterministic function of the sufficient statistics your simulator drew, the
recovery curve measures the simulator. Oracle-arm designs hit this often, and one
line of code checks it: compare the estimate to the realised truth and see
whether the difference is identically zero.

\textbf{Treat replication of a structural failure as the null it is.} Two cohorts
agreeing rules out a dataset-specific artefact. It rules out nothing about an
estimator-specific one. Conversely, find where the failure \emph{stops}
(\S\ref{sec:boundary}): a mechanism that survives every test you run is
indistinguishable from a pipeline with no sensitivity.

\textbf{Keep a statistic and its bound in the same row.} Three of the errors here
were one error: two halves of a ratio taken from different populations. A table
recording the statistic without recording which population produced it invites
the mistake, and every consumer downstream accepts the invitation.

\paragraph{The same defect, caught prospectively.} Every failure above was found
after the check reported clean. One was not, and the difference is instructive.
Planning a downstream analysis, we pre-registered a prediction of the form
``variance explained for gene X is lower than for gene Y''. A reviewer objected
before it ran: $R^2$ is a share of variance, so a gene near the assay floor loses
it to noise whatever its biology, and our two genes sit 68-fold apart in dynamic
range. Simulated with \emph{both} genes purely compositional and differing only
in abundance, the low-abundance gene returns $R^2$ falling $0.891 \rightarrow
0.000$ as the noise floor rises while the high-abundance gene holds near $0.86$
--- the prediction confirmed with nothing to find. The proposed scale-free
alternative, a log--log slope, is confounded the same way ($0.742$ against
$0.989$, collapsing to $0.023$). The fix was to judge each gene against an
\emph{abundance-matched null} of genes at its own expression level, which is
\S\ref{sec:ceiling}'s ``report the bound beside the statistic'' in a second
guise. \textbf{The distinguishing feature was not insight. It was that the
specification was written down, in a form that could be objected to, before the
numbers existed.}

\textbf{Your most-quoted number should be the one you can re-derive.} The figure
we withdrew (Appendix~\ref{app:withdrawn}) circulated internally for weeks and
shaped decisions before anyone tried to reproduce it. The producing script was
never committed.

\section*{Ethics and reproducibility}

Both cohorts are public, de-identified, previously published human tumour
single-cell datasets used under their deposited terms; the bulk cohort in
\S\ref{sec:boundary} is likewise public and de-identified. We collected no new
human data. Single-cell genotypes remain re-identifiable in principle, so we
release derived per-patient summary statistics and code rather than cell-level
matrices. We make no biological claim. The control panel in \S\ref{sec:controls}
fails, and the failure is this paper's subject. Nothing here counts as evidence
about colorectal cancer, differentiation, or any marker gene. The figure code
reads every number in this paper from a versioned result table rather than a
transcription, each table carries a commit hash and a fixed seed, and each number
got independently re-derived before reaching the text --- with the one exception
recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:withdrawn}.
