\section{The measurement, and the trap underneath it}
\label{sec:intro}

A differentiation marker falls 90\% between a patient's healthy and diseased
tissue. Two mechanisms produce the drop and they point to different
interventions. The cells making the marker have gone, a \emph{compositional}
change. Or those cells remain and produce less, an \emph{intrinsic} change. Bulk
expression cannot separate the two. Single-cell data separates them by counting
cells of the type in question in each arm and averaging their output. Write $f$
for the fraction of such cells and $m$ for their mean per-cell output. Kitagawa's
demographic standardisation \citep{kitagawa1955} splits the observed contrast:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{(f_T - f_N)\, m_N}_{\text{compositional}} \;+\;
\underbrace{f_N\, (m_T - m_N)}_{\text{intrinsic}} \;+\;
\underbrace{(f_T - f_N)(m_T - m_N)}_{\text{interaction}}
\;=\; f_T m_T - f_N m_N .
\label{eq:kitagawa}
\end{equation}
Economists call the identity Oaxaca--Blinder \citep{oaxaca1973,blinder1973},
epidemiologists call it standardisation \citep{kline2011}. The same identity sits
under synthetic control arms \citep{abadie2010} and target trial emulation
\citep{hernan2016}, so any system reporting a group-resolved quantity inherits
Equation~\ref{eq:kitagawa}'s identifiability problem rather than escaping it.
Nothing here criticises the estimator, which is arithmetic.

\paragraph{Why this identity rather than a differential-abundance method.}
Differential-abundance tools --- scCODA \citep{buettner2021}, Milo
\citep{dann2022}, DA-seq \citep{zhao2021} --- test whether a population's
\emph{share} shifts, and pseudobulk differential expression
\citep{crowell2020,squair2021} tests whether its \emph{expression} shifts. Each
answers one half. We need both halves on the same scale and reconciled against
the observed contrast, because the question is what fraction of a marker's loss
each mechanism accounts for, and Equation~\ref{eq:kitagawa} is the identity that
adds them up. cacoa \citep{petukhov2022} is the closest existing tool.
Squair et al.\ \citep{squair2021} is also why the patient rather than the cell is
our unit of inference throughout (Appendix~\ref{app:extended}); every trap below
would apply to a decomposition built on any of these.

\paragraph{The trap.} Equation~\ref{eq:kitagawa} needs $f$, and $f$ needs a
label: which cells belong to the type in question. Labelling needs marker genes.
The markers of the mature cell type \emph{are} the genes whose expression the
measurement averages, since $m$ is their mean. Using them as labels makes a
silenced cell indistinguishable from an absent one, the exact confusion the study
exists to resolve, so a leakage invariant bars them.

What remains is defining the state by the \emph{absence} of markers of the
opposite state. Everything in \S\ref{sec:depth} follows from that one forced
move, and the move is forced for any panel where a state's positive markers
double as the quantity of interest.

\paragraph{What we built, and what it was supposed to do.} The design carried five
guards, each aimed at a specific way the measurement could go wrong, and each
expected to hold under a stated precondition. Table~\ref{tab:guards} is the map
of this paper: the guard, the failure it was built to catch, and the reason it
could not. \textbf{The deliverable is \S\ref{sec:takeaways}}, which turns each
row into a question answerable before a run --- a reader who reads only
Table~\ref{tab:guards} and \S\ref{sec:takeaways} has the paper.

\paragraph{The claim.} \textbf{A check unable to fail is worse than no check,
because it turns an absence of evidence into a green light} and consumes the
attention a working check would have received. One remedy covers every failure
below: \textbf{test each check against an input that forces it to fail}, and
treat a guard with no such test as untested. Our filter throughout is
\emph{would a different group, writing correct code, still hit this?} Five
defects from our own history fail it and sit in Appendix~\ref{app:ours} as record
rather than finding. Our response, abstention as a first-class output
\citep{chow1970}, is \S\ref{sec:abstention}, and it fails its own calibration.

\paragraph{Why these are failure modes of AI \emph{in biology}.} The two
generalisable results are statistical, but what puts a project into their reach
here is assay-specific. Dropout makes a label defined by absence a reading of
sequencing depth. The populations worth resolving are rare, which is exactly
where a prevalence-bounded check goes blind. Detection floors put a control tier
below the level at which a per-cell mean means anything. And marker panels are
the mechanism that forces the whole chain: in a domain where the same genes name
a cell type and quantify it, a leakage constraint has nowhere to send the label
except to absence. None of these four is a property of the estimator. All four
are properties of the measurement.

\section{The label reads the instrument}
\label{sec:depth}

A cell gets called mature when no marker of the stem-like state appears in it.
Whether a marker appears depends on how deeply the cell was sequenced. The label
is therefore a function of the instrument as well as the biology, and the
dependence is not small. On the replication cohort the maturity call correlates
with library size at $\rho = +0.31$ against an attainable ceiling of 0.52 for a
label of its prevalence. That is 60\% of the largest correlation the statistic
takes, \emph{after} the correction described below. Normalising to counts per
10{,}000 changes nothing, because scaling never undoes a zero.

This is a shortcut cue \citep{geirhos2020}: the label is predictable from a
nuisance property of the instrument rather than the biology it names. That
detection here tracks depth and confounds with condition is established
\citep{hicks2018,luecken2019}. What is new is that the label definition is
\emph{forced} into it by a constraint the study cannot drop.

\paragraph{Why the label has to look like this, and what does not rescue it.}
The design did not pick this over an alternative. The markers of the mature state
are the genes the intrinsic term averages, so the leakage invariant bars them
from the label, and what remains is the absence of markers of the opposite state.
Two families of alternative are worth naming, because both look like escapes.

\emph{Count splitting} \citep{neufeld2023} and the data-thinning family split a
cell's UMI counts into independent folds, so the same genes can label a cell in
one fold and be tested in the other. That dissolves the leakage constraint as a
\emph{statistical} matter and is the right tool for the double-dipping problem it
was built for. It does not rescue us: splitting halves an already depth-limited
count, and the label's dependence on depth is a property of \emph{detection},
which survives the split and worsens with it. A cell too shallow to show a marker
in the full count is more so in half of it. Our constraint is not that the label
is statistically dependent on the test data. It is that the label is a reading of
how much data there was.

\emph{Reference-based probabilistic annotators} --- SingleR \citep{aran2019},
CellTypist \citep{dominguezconde2022} --- score a cell against whole expression
profiles rather than a handful of binary marker calls, which does reduce the
fragility of absence-defined labels. The leakage invariant still
applies --- the target genes must be withheld from the reference --- and what
remains of the profile is measured through the same detection process. A softer
label is not a depth-independent one. We regard this as the most promising
direction and not as a solved problem, and we did not use it here.

\paragraph{The correction, and what it costs.} Thinning each cell's marker counts
to a common depth before scoring, then refusing to score cells below the floor,
removes the mechanism instead of adjusting for it. A cell too shallow to have
shown a marker gets marked unscored, never immature. That is an abstention
verdict applied to a \emph{label}, on the same grounds as the one applied to an
estimate in \S\ref{sec:abstention}. The two arms stay incomparable at 2.36$\times$
apart in median depth, so we additionally equalise their depth distributions by
construction, keeping $\min(n_N, n_T)$ cells per depth bin. The equalisation
costs three quarters of the cells, and the surviving rows shift from 36/0/4 to
16/16/8 across \emph{estimate}, \emph{wide} and \emph{abstain}. \textbf{On this
cohort you get a clean comparison or an estimable one, never both.} The trade
belongs to the correction, not to the cohort size.

\paragraph{A number we withdrew, and what does not depend on it.} An earlier
internal report put the artefact at 46 percentage points of apparent
compositional loss, with a depth-maturity correlation of $-0.92$ and the arms
4.3$\times$ apart. Those figures were quoted internally for weeks and we
reproduced none of them; re-derived from the committed loader every sign
reverses. The producing script is absent from the repository, so the difference
resists attribution. We withdraw them. \textbf{No claim in
\S\ref{sec:ceiling}--\S\ref{sec:takeaways} depends on any withdrawn figure}: the
bound is proved and verified numerically, the tier collapse and the 49-fold
range are computed from committed per-patient tables, and the estimator
cancellation is an identity checked over every row of the sweep. What the
withdrawal costs is the magnitude of the artefact in \S\ref{sec:depth}, which we
now state only as the post-correction residual of 60\%.

\section{The confound check cannot fire where the science is}
\label{sec:ceiling}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.80\linewidth]{figures/fig_ceiling.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{A correlation-threshold confound check goes structurally blind
at both ends of its own range.} Per-arm rows on the replication cohort, one point
per patient $\times$ annotation resolution $\times$ arm.
\emph{Left:} each arm's $|\rho|$ against depth, plotted at \emph{that arm's}
prevalence, with the bound $\sqrt{3p(1-p)}$. Shaded regions are where a
tolerance of $0.20$ exceeds the bound and no data can make the check fire ---
below $p = 1.3516\%$ and, symmetrically, above $98.65\%$. \emph{Right:} the same
correlations as a share of the bound each could reach. The rarest resolution
sits at $0.375$ of its own bound against $0.184$ and $0.139$ for the two coarser
ones: normalised, it is the \emph{most} affected, which is the opposite of what
the raw statistic reports.}
\label{fig:ceiling}
\end{figure}

The obvious guard against \S\ref{sec:depth} correlates the maturity call against
sequencing depth and flags anything above a tolerance. Ours sat at
$|\rho| \geq 0.20$. The guard never fired on the rarest annotation resolution,
and a results sidecar recorded that resolution as the cleanest of four.

The guard was never going to fire. A rank correlation against a \emph{binary}
label of prevalence $p$ cannot exceed $\sqrt{3p(1-p)}$, exactly rather than
approximately, and the reason is worth stating: the maximum against a dichotomous
variable is a function of $p$ alone only once the continuous margin is pinned
down \citep{chengliu2016}, and ranks pin it down by being uniform. Pearson on raw
depth has a ceiling that depends on the depth distribution. A rank statistic, as
these diagnostics use, has one computable in a line. We verified it against
perfect separation, matching to four decimals at every prevalence tested.

\paragraph{The grid these counts are over.} One grid throughout: patients
$\times$ annotation resolutions $\times$ arms. Denominators differ because it is
filtered differently for different questions --- 48 is the depleted-arm
combinations at the rarest resolution, 64 one resolution across both annotation
axes, 190 the rows carrying a correlation at all, 166 those of them with a
defined prevalence in the arm being paired. Each count below names its filter.

Below $p = 1.3516\%$ a tolerance of 0.20 sits above the bound. Solving
$\sqrt{3p(1-p)} = 0.20$ gives $p = 0.013516$; at $1.37\%$, the figure an earlier
draft of this paper carried, the bound is $0.2013$ and the check \emph{can} still
fire --- the error this section exists to warn about, made by us, in the sentence
warning about it. Measured per arm on the replication cohort
(Figure~\ref{fig:ceiling}), \textbf{58 of 160 patient-by-resolution-by-arm rows
cannot reach the tolerance at their own prevalence}: 18 of 40 at the rarest
resolution, where the median prevalence is 4.7\%, and \textbf{40 of 40 at the
coarsest}, where every epithelial cell is called mature, the prevalence is
exactly $1.000$ and the bound is exactly zero. The bound vanishes as $p \to 1$ just as it
does as $p \to 0$: \textbf{degenerate labels are invisible at either extreme},
and the two extremes are the finest and coarsest resolutions we ran.

Normalising by the attainable bound inverts the ranking
(Figure~\ref{fig:ceiling}, right): the rarest resolution sits at 0.375 of its own
bound against 0.184 and 0.139 for the two coarser ones, so the resolution the raw
statistic called cleanest is the most affected. \textbf{The blind spot sits exactly where
the science is.} Rare populations are the ones worth resolving, and the ones this
class of check never flags.

\paragraph{Our repair inherited the defect, twice.} We fixed the diagnostic to
report the attainable bound alongside the statistic. The fix took the worst
correlation and the largest bound as \emph{independent} maxima over the two arms,
so the statistic and its bound sometimes came from different arms. Correcting the
pairing moves 66 of the 190 rows carrying a correlation, by up to 3.9$\times$,
and flips the reachability flag at the rarest resolution from ``capable of
firing'' on \textbf{64 of 64} combinations down to 16. We then made the same
error a third time inside the analysis of the fix, which put 21 of 166 rows
\emph{above their own bound}. The violation appears the moment the points are
plotted against the bound and stays invisible in a table. The diagnostic now
persists one row per arm --- correlation, bound, prevalence and depth together,
from the same cells --- so nothing downstream has to pair them. That table is what
Figure~\ref{fig:ceiling} is drawn from; before it existed the figure could show
the bound or the correlations, not both.

\section{The control panel cannot separate, and it replicates}
\label{sec:controls}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.80\linewidth]{figures/fig_tiers.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The retained-gene control gets lost as hard as the compositional
controls.} Relative intrinsic change per gene, one point per stratum, on the
primary cohort. Strata cross two quality-control floors, two population
definitions, and matched against unmatched. Tier D was chosen because disease
retains the gene, which should leave both arms alone. Tier D lands inside tier
A's range. Tier B, the intrinsic control, changes sign between strata.}
\label{fig:tiers}
\end{figure}

Our pre-registered control panel names three tiers. Genes expected to move
because cells vanished (A). Genes expected to move because cells went quiet (B).
And one chosen because disease \emph{retains} it, which should leave both arms
alone (D). Tier D is the check deciding whether the decomposition measures what
it claims.

Across the eight strata of the primary cohort, the retained-gene control moves by
$-1.000$ to $-0.951$ relative to its own baseline, against $-1.000$ to $-0.952$
for the compositional controls: tier D sits \emph{inside} tier A's range almost
exactly. The intrinsic control changes \emph{sign} between strata. Tier D, the
gene chosen because disease keeps it, gets lost as hard as the genes chosen
because disease removes them.

\paragraph{Tier D was mis-specified, and that is the finding.} The obvious
objection is that this is a panel-selection error rather than an estimator
failure, and it is worth stating plainly that the objection is correct on the
facts. Our tier-D gene retains a median $4.1\%$ of its normal-tissue per-cell
level in tumour. It is not a retained gene in this cohort; it is a strongly
depleted one, and the literature a careful reader will check says so. We chose it
from the prior expectation and did not check the depletion first. \textbf{The
control panel's job was to catch exactly that}, and what this section reports is
that it could not: a tier chosen wrongly and a tier chosen rightly produce the
same output, so the panel cannot distinguish a mis-specified control from a
broken estimator. A panel whose tiers are indistinguishable under both hypotheses
is not a weaker check than we thought. It is not a check.

\paragraph{The intrinsic control was unmeasurable from the start.} Tier B fails
for a second reason. Its genes sit at 0.04 CP10K and below in the reference arm,
against 24.9 and 19.4 for the two abundant compositional controls. The ratio
between the tiers runs from roughly 490-fold to 900-fold across the eight strata
depending on which compositional gene and which stratum is taken --- the spread
is over the numerator, not a disagreement about the tier-B level --- and holds on
the depth-matched read as well as the unmatched one, so neither the floor nor the
population definition produces it. The contrast asks the estimator to separate
0.04 from 0.02 in per-cell means, roughly one count per 250{,}000 UMIs, in a
regime where detection tracks depth. \textbf{Tier B could not have validated the
estimator whatever the biology did.}

\paragraph{The branch was pre-registered. The arm resolving it was not sound.}
Our plan named this outcome beforehand and pre-committed the reading: a broken
estimator if the harness fails too, a detection floor in the data if it passes.
The harness passes, so the branch resolves to the floor by a criterion fixed
before the result. \textbf{But the harness arm resolving it is the same arm
\S\ref{sec:oracle} shows cannot see the estimator}, so one failure supplies the
criterion settling another. A pre-committed branch is only as sound as the arm
that resolves it. Ours is real and worth having, and worth strictly less than it
appeared to be.

\paragraph{Once every tier is depleted, the algebra guarantees the collapse.}
With $\mathrm{comp} = \Delta f\, m_N$ and $\mathrm{intr} = f_N (m_T - m_N)$, the
ratio between the arms equals $(f_N/\Delta f)(m_T/m_N - 1)$. The gene enters only
through $m_T/m_N$. For a genuinely retained gene $m_T/m_N \approx 1$, the bracket
goes to zero and tier D separates cleanly --- so the collapse is \emph{not}
algebraically forced in general. It is forced once $m_T/m_N \approx 0$ for every
tier, which is an empirical fact about this panel and not a property of the
estimator. Measured per patient, the ratios then agree to within 3\% across
tiers, which is what the bracket ranging over $[-1, -0.95]$ predicts. The two
findings compose in one direction: the panel is mis-specified, and the estimator
cannot reveal a mis-specified panel, so neither is recoverable from the other.

\paragraph{And the failure replicates.} A second cohort on a different platform
with different patients shows the same pattern, agreeing to two decimal places on
the retained control. Replication across cohorts is the standard evidence a
finding is not an artefact of one dataset. Here it shows the artefact is not an
artefact of one dataset. \textbf{A structural failure replicates}, and a control
panel reproducing its own non-separation looks exactly like a control panel that
works.

\section{The same confound, in a test we had not run yet}
\label{sec:floor}

A separate arm of this work, on a different measurement platform, pre-registered
how much variance in bulk expression the mature-cell fraction explains. One panel
gene was expected to score low and a second to score high, with a fixed threshold
on the second arm. Nothing about it touches the control panel of
\S\ref{sec:controls}. It was written by different people reading different
tables, months later.

Checking the specification before locking it, we found the two genes sit
\textbf{49-fold apart in dynamic range}, at 1.72\% and 84.8\% of their
normal-tissue medians. $R^2$ is a share of variance, so a gene near the assay
floor loses it to measurement noise whatever its biology.

We simulated both genes \emph{purely compositional} --- no intrinsic component
anywhere, the same \emph{relative} patient-to-patient variation on each, so the
test starts as a coin flip. With no assay noise the low gene scores below the
high one in 51\% of 200 draws, as it should. Adding a shared additive noise
floor and changing nothing else takes the primary arm to \textbf{every draw} at a
floor of 0.5 units, and both arms to every draw at 2. \textbf{The committed
threshold had no meaning available before the run}, because how hard it bites
depends on a noise parameter nobody knows in advance. The scale-free fallback, a
log-log slope, carries the same confound. Both arms were withdrawn before the
analysis ran.

The repair is to score each gene against an abundance-matched null of off-panel
genes drawn by a rule fixed in advance, and to read its percentile within that
null rather than its raw value or its rank against another gene.

\paragraph{Why this section exists separately.} Two tests, two platforms, written
by people reading different tables, and \textbf{both compared abundance while
meaning to compare biology}. The second was caught only because the first had
taught us where to look. A single instance is a mistake. Two independent
instances of one mechanism is a failure mode.

\section{The validation curve cannot see the estimator}
\label{sec:oracle}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.80\linewidth]{figures/fig3_generator_statistic.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{A validation curve that behaves well and carries no information
about the estimator.} Ratio of recovered to true intrinsic effect, median and IQR
over replicates, for both draw pools. The curve sits near 1 wherever the
abstention rule says ``estimate'', which is what a working estimator looks like
and also what this one looks like with the estimator divided out. Applying the
estimator to the sweep's own realised summary statistics reproduces them exactly,
$\max|\hat{\imath} - \imath_{\text{realised}}| = 0$ over every row of the sweep,
leaving the ratio equal to
$\imath_{\text{realised}} / \imath_{\text{parametric}}$. The value at the
abstention point, read at the time as a two-fold over-estimate in the dangerous
direction, is a closed form with zero variance across replicates.}
\label{fig:generator}
\end{figure}

The standard way to validate an estimator like ours is simulation. Generate
samples with a known composition and a known shift, run the estimator, plot
recovered against true. Ours sat near 1 wherever the abstention rule said
``estimate'' (Figure~\ref{fig:generator}). An internal report read the curve as
the cutpoint being validated on real cells.

Simulation-based validation is standard practice and the question it answers
depends on what the estimator is a function of. Simulation-based calibration
\citep{talts2018} makes the dependence explicit by checking a property that must
hold under correct computation. A recovery curve makes no such commitment, and
ours turned out to be checking the generator.

The estimator in that arm applies Equation~\ref{eq:kitagawa} to the sample's
\emph{own} realised summary statistics, so it reproduces them exactly. Over every
row of the committed sweep, $\max|\hat{\imath} - \imath_{\text{realised}}| = 0$.
The recovery ratio therefore equals
$\imath_{\text{realised}} / \imath_{\text{parametric}}$, a comparison between
what the generator was asked for and what it drew, in which the estimator
cancels. Validating the arm with the ratio is circular.

The tell sat in the internal report and got read the wrong way. At a zero mature
fraction the ratio takes the value 2, read at the time as the estimator
over-estimating two-fold in the direction reading as a stronger finding. The
value is $1/(1-s)$ at shift $s$, a closed form with \emph{zero variance} across
replicates, taking the values 5, 2 and $4/3$ at the three shifts. A constant got
read as a measurement.

The statistic is not worthless --- the design ships a broken-estimator control
returning $0.0$, and the ratio does come out zero for it. The oracle arm by
construction is not such an estimator, and the design never says so.

\section{What we do instead, and what it costs}
\label{sec:abstention}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.80\linewidth]{figures/fig1_calibration.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Calibrating the abstention rule does not return the committed
cutpoint of 50, under either reading of an unspecified design choice.} Coverage
and discrimination against the cells available to the intrinsic comparison.
Dashed lines mark the targets fixed before the sweep, shading marks the committed
rule's three states. The two panels differ only in a draw-pool choice the design
document never makes.}
\label{fig:calibration}
\end{figure}

The response to all of it is letting the estimator refuse --- the error-reject
tradeoff \citep{chow1970} and selective classification
\citep{elyaniv2010,geifman2017}, not a bespoke choice. Our contribution is not
the reject option. It is that \textbf{calibrating the reject threshold is where
this broke}, with the same shape as the checks above.

Our decomposition returns one of three verdicts per patient, gene and resolution:
an estimate, a wide-interval estimate, or \notest{}, where the intrinsic term is
\texttt{None} and never $0.0$. Zero and ``we did not ask'' are the two values a
downstream average cannot tell apart. Abstention dominates where the
compositional question gets sharpest. At the finest resolution 28 of 28 patients
abstain under one population definition and 24 of 28 under the other.

\paragraph{The rule's own calibration fails.} Three acceptance criteria were
fixed before any sweep ran: a detectable halving of per-cell output, 90\%
interval coverage, and 80\% discrimination. Running the procedure on held-out
real cells, the committed cutpoint of 50 cells is not what comes back
(Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}). Under the draw pool our sweep code implements, no
tested count meets both criteria and the routine returns nothing at all. Under a
second reading of the same unspecified choice it returns 100 --- and then 90,
once the grid carries points in the interval where the criteria actually cross,
because the committed grid varies a fraction against a fixed cell budget and can
reach no count between 40 and 100. \textbf{A grid that cannot resolve a crossing
still returns a stable number}, which is \S\ref{sec:oracle}'s failure in a second
statistic.

\paragraph{And the calibration table scored refusals as failures.} Its lowest bin
merged replicates holding \emph{no} interval, the abstention case, with those
holding one. Comparisons against \texttt{NaN} return false, so undefined
intervals counted as coverage \emph{and} discrimination failures: the published
row read $(0.36, 0.31)$ where the defined replicates give $(0.72, 0.62)$, exactly
half. \textbf{An abstention silently counted as a failure, inside the routine
calibrating the rule built to prevent exactly this} --- arriving as a boolean in
a rate rather than a number in a column, where the writer's assertion never sees
it.

\section{What to take from this}
\label{sec:takeaways}

Each of these is a question answerable before a run, and each maps to a section
above. The general form is the one sentence in \S\ref{sec:intro}: test every
check against an input that forces it to fail.

\textbf{Check whether your label is defined by the absence of a signal}
(\S\ref{sec:depth}). If a state's positive markers are also the quantity you
average over, a leakage constraint forces the label to be defined by absence, and
absence reads dropout rather than biology. That is answerable at design time,
from the panel alone, before any data exists. Count splitting does not remove it
and halving the counts makes it worse; a softer, profile-based label is the
direction we would take next.

\textbf{Report the attainable bound beside every threshold check}
(\S\ref{sec:ceiling}). A rank correlation against a binary label of prevalence
$p$ never exceeds $\sqrt{3p(1-p)}$. A threshold above that bound is a check
unable to fail. Where the bound sits below the threshold, the honest output is
\emph{not testable} rather than \emph{clean}, an abstention verdict for the check
on the same grounds as one for an estimate. Keep the statistic and its bound in
the same row, over the same population, or a later consumer will pair them
wrongly. Ours did, three times.

\textbf{Check that your control panel's tiers are actually different, on your
data, before you run it} (\S\ref{sec:controls}). Ours could not separate, for two
compounding reasons: a tier was mis-specified, and the estimator's algebra makes
mis-specified and correct tiers produce the same output once every gene is
depleted. Both were available from the panel and one baseline table, with no
decomposition. The question costs an afternoon and we never asked. And treat
replication of a structural failure as the null it is: two cohorts agreeing rules
out a dataset-specific artefact and nothing about an estimator-specific one.

\textbf{Judge a per-gene statistic against an abundance-matched null, not against
another gene} (\S\ref{sec:floor}). Draw off-panel genes at the outcome gene's own
expression level by a rule fixed in advance, then read where the gene falls in
that distribution. A contrast between two genes at different abundances measures
abundance.

\textbf{Ask what your validation statistic is a function of}
(\S\ref{sec:oracle}). If your estimator is a deterministic function of the
sufficient statistics your simulator drew, the recovery curve measures the
simulator. Oracle-arm designs hit this often, and one line of code checks it:
compare the estimate to the realised truth and see whether the difference is
identically zero.

\textbf{State what a rate did with its abstentions} (\S\ref{sec:abstention}).
Any rate computed over a verdict set has a denominator question, and the answer
belongs beside the rate. A comparison against a missing value returns false in
most numerical stacks, so an abstention lands silently in the failure count. That
halved a published row of ours, inside the routine calibrating the abstention
rule.

\textbf{Audit the dependency graph between your checks} (\S\ref{sec:controls}).
A pre-committed branch is only as sound as the arm that resolves it. Ours was
resolved by a statistic \S\ref{sec:oracle} shows could not see the estimator, so
one blind check licensed a conclusion about another. Write down which check
each of your commitments depends on, and the graph will usually be shorter and
more circular than it felt while building it.

\section*{Ethics and reproducibility}

Both cohorts are public, de-identified, previously published human tumour
single-cell datasets used under their deposited terms. We collected no new human
data. Single-cell genotypes remain re-identifiable in principle, so anything we
release is derived per-patient summary statistics and code rather than cell-level
matrices. The control panel in \S\ref{sec:controls} fails, and the failure is
this paper's subject. Nothing here counts as evidence about colorectal cancer,
differentiation, or any marker gene. The figure code reads every number in this
paper from a versioned result table rather than a transcription, each table
carries a commit hash and a fixed seed, and each number got independently
re-derived before reaching the text. Where a number could not be re-derived we
withdrew it and said so (\S\ref{sec:depth}).
