\section{The measurement, and the trap underneath it}
\label{sec:intro}

A differentiation marker falls 90\% between a patient's healthy and diseased
tissue. Two mechanisms produce the drop and they point to different
interventions. The cells making the marker have gone, a \emph{compositional}
change. Or those cells remain and produce less, an \emph{intrinsic} change. Bulk
expression cannot separate the two. Single-cell data separates them by counting
cells of the type in question in each arm and averaging their output. Write $f$
for the fraction of such cells and $m$ for their mean per-cell output. Kitagawa's
demographic standardisation \citep{kitagawa1955} splits the observed contrast:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{(f_T - f_N)\, m_N}_{\text{compositional}} \;+\;
\underbrace{f_N\, (m_T - m_N)}_{\text{intrinsic}} \;+\;
\underbrace{(f_T - f_N)(m_T - m_N)}_{\text{interaction}}
\;=\; f_T m_T - f_N m_N .
\label{eq:kitagawa}
\end{equation}
Economists call the identity Oaxaca--Blinder \citep{oaxaca1973,blinder1973},
epidemiologists call it standardisation \citep{kline2011}. The same identity sits
under synthetic control arms \citep{abadie2010} and target trial emulation
\citep{hernan2016}, so any system reporting a group-resolved quantity inherits
Equation~\ref{eq:kitagawa}'s identifiability problem rather than escaping it.
Nothing here criticises the estimator, which is arithmetic.

\paragraph{Why this identity rather than a differential-abundance method.}
Differential-abundance tools --- scCODA \citep{buettner2021}, Milo
\citep{dann2022}, DA-seq \citep{zhao2021} --- test whether a population's
\emph{share} shifts; pseudobulk differential expression
\citep{crowell2020,squair2021} tests whether its \emph{expression} shifts. Each
answers one half, and the question is what fraction of a marker's loss each
mechanism accounts for, so we need both halves on one scale and reconciled
against the observed contrast. cacoa \citep{petukhov2022} is the closest existing
tool. \citet{squair2021} is also why the patient rather than the cell is our unit
of inference throughout (Appendix~\ref{app:extended}); every trap below would
apply to a decomposition built on any of these.

\paragraph{The trap.} Equation~\ref{eq:kitagawa} needs $f$, and $f$ needs a
label: which cells belong to the type in question. Labelling needs marker genes.
The markers of the mature cell type \emph{are} the genes whose expression the
measurement averages, since $m$ is their mean. Using them as labels makes a
silenced cell indistinguishable from an absent one, the exact confusion the study
exists to resolve, so a leakage invariant bars them.

What remains is defining the state by the \emph{absence} of markers of the
opposite state. Everything in \S\ref{sec:depth} follows from that one forced
move, and the move is forced for any panel where a state's positive markers
double as the quantity of interest.

\paragraph{What we built, and what it was supposed to do.} The design carried five
guards, each aimed at a specific way the measurement could go wrong, and each
expected to hold under a stated precondition. Table~\ref{tab:guards} is the map
of this paper: the guard, the failure it was built to catch, and the reason it
could not. \textbf{The deliverable is \S\ref{sec:takeaways}}, which turns each
row into a question answerable before a run --- a reader who reads only
Table~\ref{tab:guards} and \S\ref{sec:takeaways} has the paper.

\paragraph{The claim.} \textbf{A check unable to fail is worse than no check,
because it turns an absence of evidence into a green light} and consumes the
attention a working check would have received. One remedy covers every failure
below: \textbf{test each check against an input that forces it to fail}, and
treat a guard with no such test as untested. That is mutation testing's premise
\citep{demillo1978,jia2011} moved from unit tests to analysis diagnostics, and
its closest precedent in machine learning is \citet{adebayo2018}, where a
saliency check surviving the randomisation of the model it explains was never
reading the model. Our filter throughout is
\emph{would a different group, writing correct code, still hit this?} Five
defects from our own history fail it and sit in Appendix~\ref{app:ours} as record
rather than finding. Our response, abstention as a first-class output
\citep{chow1970}, is \S\ref{sec:abstention}, and it fails its own calibration.

\paragraph{Why these are failure modes of AI \emph{in biology}.} The two
generalisable results are statistical, but what puts a project within their reach
is assay-specific: dropout makes a label defined by absence a reading of depth,
the populations worth resolving are rare and that is where a prevalence-bounded
check goes blind, and detection floors put a control tier below the level at
which a per-cell mean means anything. Marker panels force the chain: where the
same genes name a cell type and quantify it, a leakage constraint has nowhere to
send the label except absence. None of the four is a property of the estimator;
all four are properties of the measurement. One thing here is a property of
neither: a language-model assistant wrote most of the code these guards live in.
That changes how fast checks appear and leaves untouched which of them can fail,
and two of Appendix~\ref{app:ours}'s defects came from it --- the statement after
the bibliography gives the arrangement in full.

\section{The label reads the instrument}
\label{sec:depth}

A cell gets called mature when no marker of the stem-like state appears in it.
Whether a marker appears depends on how deeply the cell was sequenced. The
label is therefore a function of the instrument as well as the biology, and the
dependence is not small. On the replication cohort (GSE132465, $n = 10$), at
the middle annotation resolution, the maturity call correlates with library
size at a median $|\rho| = 0.26$ in the reference arm --- above the $0.20$
tolerance on 12 of its 20 patient-by-axis rows, and a third of the bound that
arm's own prevalence permits --- \emph{after} the correction described below.
We report it per arm because a max-over-arms summary cannot be paired with a
prevalence (\S\ref{sec:ceiling}). Normalising to counts per 10{,}000 changes
nothing, because scaling never undoes a zero.

This is a shortcut cue \citep{geirhos2020}: the label is predictable from a
nuisance property of the instrument rather than the biology it names. That
detection here tracks depth and confounds with condition is established
\citep{hicks2018,luecken2019}. What is new is that the label definition is
\emph{forced} into it by a constraint the study cannot drop.

\paragraph{Why the label has to look like this, and what does not rescue it.}
The design did not pick this over an alternative. The markers of the mature state
are the genes the intrinsic term averages, so the leakage invariant bars them
from the label, and what remains is the absence of markers of the opposite
state. Three families of alternative look like escapes and are not. Count splitting
\citep{neufeld2023} and data thinning dissolve the leakage constraint as a
\emph{statistical} matter, but halve an already depth-limited count, and the
dependence at issue is on \emph{detection}, which survives the split and worsens
with it. Reference-based probabilistic annotators --- SingleR \citep{aran2019},
CellTypist \citep{dominguezconde2022} --- soften the label but still read it
through the same detection process, and the leakage invariant still withholds the
target genes from the reference. We regard the second as the most promising
direction and did not use it here. Appendix~\ref{app:alternatives} gives all in
full.

\paragraph{The correction, and what it costs.} Thinning each cell's marker counts
to a common depth before scoring, then refusing to score cells below the floor,
removes the mechanism instead of adjusting for it: a cell too shallow to have
shown a marker is marked unscored, never immature --- an abstention verdict
applied to a \emph{label}. The arms stay incomparable at 2.36$\times$ apart in
median depth, so we also equalise their depth distributions by construction,
keeping $\min(n_N, n_T)$ cells per depth bin. The equalisation
costs three quarters of the cells, and the surviving rows shift from 36/0/4 to
16/16/8 across \emph{estimate}, \emph{wide} and \emph{abstain}. \textbf{On this
cohort you get a clean comparison or an estimable one, never both.} The trade
belongs to the correction, not to the cohort size.

\paragraph{A number we withdrew.} An earlier internal report put the artefact at
46 percentage points of apparent compositional loss, with a depth-maturity
correlation of $-0.92$ and the arms 4.3$\times$ apart. Re-derived from the
committed loader every sign reverses, and the producing script is absent from
the repository, so the difference resists attribution. We withdraw all three.
\textbf{No claim below depends on a withdrawn figure}, and what the withdrawal
costs is the magnitude here, now stated only as the residual above.

\section{The confound check is bounded by prevalence, and cannot say so}
\label{sec:ceiling}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_ceiling.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{A correlation-threshold confound check goes structurally blind
at both ends of its own range.} Per-arm rows on the replication cohort.
\emph{Left:} each arm's $|\rho|$ against depth at \emph{that arm's} prevalence,
with the bound $\sqrt{3p(1-p)}$; shading marks where a tolerance of $0.20$
exceeds the bound, below $p = 1.3516\%$ and above $98.65\%$. \emph{Right:} the
same as a share of each row's own bound, over the rows where that ratio is
defined --- 22 of 40 at the rarest resolution against 40 of 40 at the two
middle, so the three medians are not over comparable populations.}
\label{fig:ceiling}
\end{figure}

The obvious guard against \S\ref{sec:depth} correlates the maturity call against
sequencing depth and flags anything above a tolerance. Ours sat at
$|\rho| \geq 0.20$. It never fired on the rarest annotation resolution, and a
results sidecar recorded that resolution as the cleanest of four.

It was never going to fire. A rank correlation against a \emph{binary} label of
prevalence $p$ cannot exceed $\sqrt{3p(1-p)}$, exactly rather than approximately:
the maximum against a dichotomous variable is a function of $p$ alone once the
continuous margin is pinned down \citep{chengliu2016}, and ranks pin it down by
being uniform. Pearson on raw depth has a ceiling that depends on the depth
distribution; a rank statistic, as these diagnostics use, has one computable in a
line. We verified it against perfect separation, matching to four decimals at
every prevalence tested.

Below $p = 1.3516\%$ a tolerance of 0.20 sits above the bound. Solving
$\sqrt{3p(1-p)} = 0.20$ gives $p = 0.013516$; at $1.37\%$, the figure an earlier
draft of this paper carried, the bound is $0.2013$ and the check \emph{can} still
fire --- the error this section exists to warn about, made by us, in the sentence
warning about it.

\paragraph{Where the bound bites, and where it does not.} On our own labels
\textbf{58 of 160 per-arm rows cannot reach the tolerance, and every one is
degenerate rather than rare}: 18 at $p$ exactly $0$, 40 at $p$ exactly $1$. That
a threshold cannot fire on a label with no variance is arithmetic, not a finding
(the bound itself is proved in Appendix~\ref{app:bound}). \textbf{But the regime
is empty in our labels, not in this data.} Scored the same way --- same cells,
same depth, same statistic --- both cohorts' \emph{published} subtype
annotations put \textbf{570 per-arm rows in $0 < p < 1.3516\%$}, 250 of them
above half their attainable bound and one at $0.994$ of it. The tolerance flags
\textbf{none}; where it can fire it flags 26\% (Appendix~\ref{app:rare}).

\paragraph{What the degenerate rows do show is \S\ref{sec:abstention}'s failure.}
At $p \in \{0,1\}$ the rank correlation is \emph{undefined}, not small. Our
diagnostic reported the label as not tracking depth on all 58, because an
undefined statistic had nowhere to land except the negative verdict. \textbf{A
check with no abstention state reports absence of evidence as evidence of
absence} --- the coercion of \S\ref{sec:abstention}, in a second routine, written
by different people months earlier. We reported these as two failures in two
sections and did not see they were one until we set them side by side.

\paragraph{Our repair inherited the defect, twice.} We fixed the diagnostic to
report the attainable bound alongside the statistic. The fix took the worst
correlation and the largest bound as \emph{independent} maxima over the two arms,
so the statistic and its bound sometimes came from different arms. Correcting the
pairing flips the reachability flag at the rarest resolution from ``capable of
firing'' on \textbf{64 of 64} combinations down to 16
(Appendix~\ref{app:collapse}). We then made the same error a third time inside
the analysis of the fix, which put rows
\emph{above their own bound} --- a violation visible the moment the points are
plotted and invisible in a table. The diagnostic now persists one row per arm,
correlation and bound and prevalence and depth from the same cells, so nothing
downstream has to pair them. That table is what Figure~\ref{fig:ceiling} is drawn
from; before it existed the figure could show the bound or the correlations, not
both.

Normalising by the attainable bound inverts the ranking on the rows carrying a
ratio (Figure~\ref{fig:ceiling}, right): 0.375 at the rarest resolution against
0.184 and 0.139 for the two middle. \textbf{The denominators differ, and that
weakens it} --- 0.375 is the median over the 22 rows with a defined ratio, the
other 18 being the degenerate ones above, while the others run over all 40. One
resolution's surviving rows against another's complete ones is the fourth time
in this section that a statistic and its denominator came apart.

\section{The control panel cannot separate, and it replicates}
\label{sec:controls}

Our pre-registered control panel names three tiers. Genes expected to move
because cells vanished (A). Genes expected to move because cells went quiet (B).
And one chosen because disease \emph{retains} it, which should leave both arms
alone (D). Tier D is the check deciding whether the decomposition measures what
it claims.

Across the eight strata of the primary cohort, the retained-gene control moves by
$-1.000$ to $-0.951$ relative to its own baseline, against $-1.000$ to $-0.952$
for the compositional controls: tier D sits \emph{inside} tier A's range almost
exactly. The intrinsic control changes \emph{sign} between strata. Tier D, the
gene chosen because disease keeps it, gets lost as hard as the genes chosen
because disease removes them.

\paragraph{Tier D was mis-specified, and that is the finding.} The obvious
objection is that this is a panel-selection error rather than an estimator
failure, and on the facts the objection is correct. Our tier-D gene, MS4A12,
retains a median $4.1\%$ of its normal-tissue per-cell level in tumour: not a
retained gene in this cohort but a strongly depleted one, as the literature a
careful reader will check says. We chose it from the prior expectation and never
checked the depletion. \textbf{The control panel's job was to catch exactly
that}, and it could not: a tier chosen wrongly and a tier chosen rightly produce
the same output, so the panel cannot tell a mis-specified control from a broken
estimator. A panel whose tiers are indistinguishable under both hypotheses is not
a weaker check than we thought. It is not a check.

\paragraph{The intrinsic control was unmeasurable from the start.} Tier B fails
for a second reason. Its genes sit at 0.04 CP10K and below in the reference arm
(MLH1 at 0.040, SFRP1 and SFRP2 at zero), against 25.0 and 17.9 for GUCA2A and
GUCA2B, the two abundant compositional controls, on the depth-matched middle
resolution. The ratio between the tiers is never below 200-fold in any of the
eight strata, on the matched read or the unmatched one, so neither the floor nor
the population definition produces it. The contrast asks the estimator to separate
0.04 from 0.02 in per-cell means, roughly one count per 250{,}000 UMIs, in a
regime where detection tracks depth. \textbf{Tier B could not have validated
the estimator whatever the biology did.}

\paragraph{The branch was pre-registered. The arm resolving it was not sound.}
Our plan pre-committed the reading: a broken estimator if the harness fails too,
a detection floor in the data if it passes. The harness passes, so the branch
resolves to the floor by a criterion fixed before the result. \textbf{But that
arm is the one \S\ref{sec:oracle} shows cannot see the estimator}, so one failure
supplies the criterion settling another. A pre-committed branch is only as sound
as the arm resolving it: ours is worth having, and worth strictly less than it
appeared to be.

\paragraph{Once every tier is depleted, the algebra guarantees the collapse.}
The ratio between the arms is $(f_N/\Delta f)(m_T/m_N - 1)$, so the gene enters
only through $m_T/m_N$: a genuinely retained gene has $m_T/m_N \approx 1$ and
separates cleanly, and the collapse is \emph{not} algebraically forced in
general (Appendix~\ref{app:collapse}). It is forced once $m_T/m_N \approx 0$ for
every tier, which is an empirical fact about this panel rather than a property
of the estimator. The two findings compose in one direction: the panel is
mis-specified, and the estimator cannot reveal a mis-specified panel, so neither
is recoverable from the other.

\paragraph{And the failure replicates.} A second cohort with different patients,
a different centre and a different chemistry shows the same pattern, agreeing to
two decimal places on the retained control. Replication is the standard evidence
that a finding is not an artefact of one dataset; here it shows the artefact is
not. \textbf{A structural failure replicates}, and a control panel reproducing
its own non-separation looks exactly like one that works.

\section{A threshold with no meaning available before the run}
\label{sec:floor}

A bulk arm (TCGA-COAD and GSE39582), written by different people months later,
pre-registered how much variance in bulk expression the mature-cell fraction
explains: GUCA2A to score low, CDX2 high, against a fixed threshold. The two sit
\textbf{49-fold apart in dynamic range}, at 1.72\% and 84.8\% of their
normal-tissue medians. $R^2$ is a share of variance, so a gene near the assay
floor loses it to noise whatever its biology, and simulating both \emph{purely
compositional} reproduces the committed prediction on noise alone
(Appendix~\ref{app:floor}). \textbf{No value of that threshold had a meaning
available before the run}: how hard it bites depends on a noise parameter nobody
knows in advance. Both arms were withdrawn before the analysis ran.

\section{The validation curve cannot see the estimator}
\label{sec:oracle}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig3_generator_statistic.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{A validation curve that behaves well and carries no information
about the estimator.} Ratio of recovered to true intrinsic effect, median and IQR
over replicates, both draw pools. It sits near 1 wherever the rule says
``estimate'' --- what a working estimator looks like, and also what this one
looks like with the estimator divided out.}
\label{fig:generator}
\end{figure}

The standard way to validate an estimator like ours is simulation. Generate
samples with a known composition and a known shift, run the estimator, plot
recovered against true. Ours sat near 1 wherever the abstention rule said
``estimate'' (Figure~\ref{fig:generator}). An internal report read the curve as
the cutpoint being validated on real cells.

What a simulation answers depends on what the estimator is a function of.
Simulation-based calibration \citep{talts2018} makes that dependence explicit by
checking a property that must hold under correct computation; a recovery curve
makes no such commitment. The estimator in that arm applies
Equation~\ref{eq:kitagawa} to the sample's \emph{own} realised summary
statistics, so it reproduces them exactly --- over every row of the committed
sweep, $\max|\hat{\imath} - \imath_{\text{realised}}| = 0$. The ratio therefore
equals $\imath_{\text{realised}} / \imath_{\text{parametric}}$, a comparison
between what the generator was asked for and what it drew, in which the estimator
cancels. Validating the arm with it is circular.

The tell sat in the internal report and was read the wrong way. At a zero mature
fraction the ratio takes the value 2, read at the time as a two-fold
over-estimate in the direction of a stronger finding. It is $1/(1-s)$ at shift
$s$, a closed form with \emph{zero variance} across replicates, taking the values
5, 2 and $4/3$ at the three shifts. A constant got read as a measurement.

The statistic is not worthless: the design ships a broken-estimator control
returning $0.0$, and the ratio does come out zero for it. The oracle arm by
construction is not such an estimator, and the design never says so.

\section{What we do instead, and what it costs}
\label{sec:abstention}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig1_calibration.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Calibrating the abstention rule does not return the committed
cutpoint of 50, under either reading of an unspecified design choice.} Dashed
lines mark the targets fixed before the sweep, shading the committed rule's three
states. The panels differ only in a draw-pool choice the design never makes.}
\label{fig:calibration}
\end{figure}

The response to all of it is letting the estimator refuse --- the error-reject
tradeoff \citep{chow1970} and selective classification
\citep{elyaniv2010,geifman2017,angelopoulos2023}, not a bespoke choice. Our contribution is not
the reject option but that \textbf{calibrating the reject threshold is where this
broke}, with the same shape as the checks above.

Our decomposition returns one of three verdicts per patient, gene and resolution,
gated on the mature-cell count $n$: an estimate at $n \geq 50$, a wide-interval
estimate at $20 \leq n < 50$, and \notest{} below, where the intrinsic term is
\texttt{None} and never $0.0$. Zero and ``we did not ask'' are the two values a
downstream average cannot tell apart. Abstention dominates where the
compositional question gets sharpest. At the rarest resolution 28 of 28 patients
abstain under one population definition and 24 of 28 under the other.

\paragraph{What the reject option buys, and which part of it.} Refusing is
trivially achievable by refusing always, so the gate must be scored where the
estimand exists and where it does not. We scored it against four alternatives on
synthetic cells with closed-form truth, one world setting the diseased mature
fraction to zero so the intrinsic term has no referent
(Table~\ref{tab:bench}, 200 replicates per world). The generator is exactly the
model Equation~\ref{eq:kitagawa} assumes, so this tests the gate and not
robustness to misspecification; the load-bearing comparison is the ablation.

Ungated methods invent large effects where there is nothing to estimate: on a
scale where the reference per-cell mean is 20.0, the ablation invents a median
$|$intrinsic$|$ of 7.99 and the naive difference reports the entire drop as
silencing in cells that are not there. \textbf{The
second result went against us.} A width gate --- abstain when the interval is too
wide to resolve the effect the design names --- refuses all 200 of the same
undefined cases, for a false confidence of 0.00 like ours, and costs no
detections at all against our 0.853. It refuses in that world and in no other,
so the width criterion never binds and the gate reduces to its \emph{finiteness}
guard. \textbf{The only part of any gate that does work here is the check that
the estimand exists at all}, and our count cutpoint's 88 extra refusals buy
nothing measurable. That guard is one line and easy to omit: \texttt{nan >
threshold} is false, so the obvious implementation declines to abstain in exactly
the world the estimand does not exist.

\paragraph{This cohort cannot calibrate the rule.} Three acceptance criteria were
fixed before any sweep ran: a detectable halving of per-cell output, 90\%
coverage of the parametric truth by the bootstrap interval, and 80\%
discrimination --- the share of replicates whose interval excludes zero. Running the procedure on held-out
real cells, the committed cutpoint of 50 cells is not what comes back
(Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}). Under the draw pool our sweep code implements, no
tested count meets both criteria and the routine returns nothing at all. Under a
second reading of the same unspecified choice it returns 100 --- and then 90,
once the grid carries points in the interval where the criteria actually cross,
because the committed grid varies a fraction against a fixed cell budget and can
reach no count between 40 and 100. Neither number is as solid as it reads.
\textbf{Across seeds it is the grid that \emph{can} resolve the crossing that is
the less stable of the two}: at 500 replicates the coarse grid returns 100 on 8
of 8 seeds against 90 on 7 of 8 for the filled one, and at 50 replicates the
filled grid wanders between 45 and 90 while the coarse one still returns 100 on
12 of 13. A grid unable to resolve a crossing still returns a stable number ---
the stability was the coarseness --- which is \S\ref{sec:oracle}'s failure in a
second statistic. With ten patients admitting 45 distinct holdout pairs, the
claim this supports is that \textbf{this cohort cannot calibrate the cutpoint},
not that the true value is 90.

\paragraph{And the calibration table scored refusals as failures.} Its lowest bin
merged replicates holding \emph{no} interval, the abstention case, with those
holding one. Comparisons against \texttt{NaN} return false, so
an undefined interval counted as a coverage \emph{and} a discrimination failure,
halving both rates in that bin. \textbf{An abstention silently counted as a
failure, inside the routine calibrating the rule built to prevent exactly this}
--- arriving as a boolean in a rate rather than a number in a column, where the
writer's assertion never sees it.

\section{What to take from this}
\label{sec:takeaways}

Each is answerable before a run, and each maps to a section above. The general
form is \S\ref{sec:intro}'s one sentence: test every check against an input that
forces it to fail.

\textbf{Check whether your label is defined by the absence of a signal}
(\S\ref{sec:depth}). If a state's positive markers are also the quantity you
average over, a leakage constraint forces the label into absence, and absence
reads dropout rather than biology. That is answerable from the panel alone,
before any data exists. Count splitting does not remove it and halving the counts
makes it worse; a softer, profile-based label is where we would go next.

\textbf{Pick a statistic without a prevalence ceiling} (\S\ref{sec:ceiling}). A
rank correlation against a binary label of prevalence $p$ never exceeds
$\sqrt{3p(1-p)}$, so a fixed threshold on it is unfalsifiable on rare labels. The
rank-biserial correlation $2\,\mathrm{AUC} - 1$ carries no such ceiling --- it
reaches 1 under perfect separation at any $p$ --- and label-versus-covariate
diagnostics in this field already work this way
\citep{buttner2019,korsunsky2019,luecken2022}. We used neither. Keep a statistic
and its bound in one row over one population; ours came apart four times.

\textbf{Check that your control panel's tiers are actually different, on your
data, before you run it} (\S\ref{sec:controls}). Ours could not separate for two
compounding reasons: a tier was mis-specified, and the algebra makes
mis-specified and correct tiers produce the same output once every gene is
depleted. Both were available from the panel and one baseline table, and we never
asked. And treat replication of a structural failure as the null it is: two
cohorts agreeing rules out a dataset-specific artefact, not an
estimator-specific one.

\textbf{Judge a per-gene statistic against an abundance-matched null, not against
another gene} (\S\ref{sec:floor}). Draw off-panel genes at the outcome gene's own
expression level by a rule fixed in advance, then read where the gene falls in
that distribution. A contrast between two genes at different abundances measures
abundance.

\textbf{Ask what your validation statistic is a function of}
(\S\ref{sec:oracle}). If your estimator is a deterministic function of the
sufficient statistics your simulator drew, the recovery curve measures the
simulator. One line checks it: compare the estimate to the realised truth and see
whether the difference is identically zero.

\textbf{Give undefined its own verdict, and say what a rate did with it}
(\S\ref{sec:ceiling}, \S\ref{sec:abstention}). A comparison against a missing
value returns false in most numerical stacks, so an undefined statistic lands
silently in the negative verdict and an abstention lands silently in a failure
count. \textbf{We hit this twice} --- in a confound diagnostic and in a
calibration routine written months apart --- and reported it as two failures
before noticing it was one. And score the gate you propose against an ablation of
itself: ours bought what one existence check already bought.

\textbf{Audit the dependency graph between your checks} (\S\ref{sec:controls}).
A pre-committed branch is only as sound as the arm resolving it, and ours was
resolved by a statistic \S\ref{sec:oracle} shows could not see the estimator.
Write down what each commitment depends on; the graph is usually shorter and more
circular than it felt.

\section*{Ethics and reproducibility}

Both single-cell cohorts are public, de-identified, previously published human
tumour datasets used under their deposited terms: GSE178341 (32 patients with
matched normal tissue, the primary cohort) and GSE132465 (10 of 23 patients
carrying a matched normal--tumour pair, the replication cohort), both 10x
Chromium colorectal cancer atlases. The bulk arm of \S\ref{sec:floor} uses
TCGA-COAD and GSE39582. Panel genes named in the text are those frozen in the
project's configuration before any analysis code existed. We collected no new
human data. Single-cell genotypes remain re-identifiable in principle, so
anything we release is derived per-patient summary statistics and code rather
than cell-level matrices. The control panel in \S\ref{sec:controls} fails, and
the failure is this paper's subject. Nothing here counts as evidence about
colorectal cancer, differentiation, or any marker gene. The figure code reads
every number in this paper from a versioned result table rather than a
transcription, each table carries a commit hash and a fixed seed, and each
number got independently re-derived before reaching the text. Where a number
could not be re-derived we withdrew it and said so (\S\ref{sec:depth}).
